Chinese University Transcript Translation: English Version, Certified Translation or Notarization?

Compare university-issued English transcripts, professional translations, CSSD verification and recipient-specific overseas submission requirements.

By Global Attest Document Services Team · Published: September 6, 2026
Direct answer: “English transcript,” “certified translation,” “notarized translation” and “CSSD verification report” are not interchangeable. The university issues the academic record, a translator changes its language, CSSD verifies an applicable record, and the final recipient decides which combination and delivery route it accepts.

If your Chinese university transcript is not in the language required by an overseas university, credential evaluator, immigration authority or employer, you may need an English version or a professional translation.

Before ordering anything, ask four questions:

  1. Which source transcript is required?
  2. Which language must it be in?
  3. Who may prepare or certify the translation?
  4. Who must send each document, and through which route?

If you do not yet have the underlying transcript, first review how to obtain an old Chinese university transcript after graduation.

First Identify the Transcript You Have

Before arranging translation, determine what the source record actually is. It may be:

  • an official Chinese transcript issued by the university;
  • a university-issued English or bilingual transcript;
  • an archive-certified transcript or academic-record extract;
  • an official electronic transcript;
  • a student-portal download;
  • a photocopy or scan of an official transcript; or
  • an unofficial grade report or screenshot.

These versions do not necessarily have the same status. An ordinary scan preserves visible content, but it does not automatically become an official transcript or satisfy a direct-delivery requirement. A portal screenshot may help identify courses and grades without being acceptable as the final academic record.

Can the University Issue an English or Bilingual Transcript?

Some Chinese universities publish procedures for English or bilingual transcripts. This is institution-specific rather than a nationwide entitlement.

Published procedures illustrate the variation. Beijing Language and Culture University’s archive guidance describes institution-specific academic-record services. Tsinghua University’s graduate transcript procedure includes Chinese and English transcript options under its stated process. The University of Shanghai for Science and Technology archive procedure and Tongji University’s published transcript information provide further institution-specific examples.

These examples do not prove that every university provides an English transcript, serves every former student remotely or offers the same sealing and delivery options.

Can Alumni Order an English Transcript After Graduation?

Sometimes. A university may offer alumni ordering, archive services or a procedure for former international students. Availability may depend on the graduation year, program, degree level, existing data and identity-verification method.

If the university does not offer an English version, ask the recipient whether it accepts the official Chinese transcript accompanied by a professional translation. Former international students can also review document services for international students and former graduates.

Does a University English Transcript Replace the Chinese Original?

Not automatically. A university-issued English transcript may be an official academic record in its own right, but the recipient may still request the Chinese transcript, an independently translated version of the original documentation or a particular verification report.

Do not assume that “issued in English” and “meets the recipient’s translation requirements” mean the same thing. Confirm the required source document and language version before ordering.

What Is an Independent Professional Translation?

An independent professional translation converts the visible content of the source transcript into another language. It does not change who issued the academic record.

A complete translation normally needs to reproduce the university and student details, program, major, study period, course names, credits or hours, grades, grading explanations, seals, annotations, dates and document identifiers.

The translator should not correct grades, fill gaps, reinterpret results or recreate information missing from the source. If the source contains an apparent error or incomplete entry, the university—not the translator—must determine whether its record can be corrected or clarified.

A recipient may request a certification statement, translator’s name, signature, contact details, qualifications, date or company seal. Those requirements come from the recipient or applicable procedure; China does not have one translation format automatically accepted everywhere.

Can I Translate the Transcript Myself?

You may prepare a personal translation for reference, but a recipient may reject a translation prepared by the applicant or a family member.

WES’s official translation requirements state that requested translations must be exact, clear, legible and professionally prepared. Applicant-prepared, handwritten or incomplete translations are not acceptable under that guidance. Other recipients may apply different standards.

What Does a Translation Company Stamp Prove?

A translation-company seal or certification statement may identify the business that prepared the translation and confirm its responsibility for the translated text. It does not, by itself, authenticate the university transcript, confirm the grades, replace the university seal, constitute CSSD verification, create a notarial certificate or guarantee overseas acceptance.

A recipient may accept a company-certified translation, require a named translator’s declaration or ask for notarization.

Certified Translation and Notarized Translation Are Different

“Certified translation” is a recipient-dependent term. It may refer to a translation accompanied by a signed accuracy statement, translator credentials, contact information or a translation-company certification. The precise standard varies between countries and organizations.

Notarization is a separate process performed by a notary office. Depending on the requested notarial matter, it may concern an academic document, conformity between a copy and an original, a signature or declaration, a translation connected to a notarial certificate, or a foreign-related notarial document containing translated pages.

A notarized translation is therefore not the same as a university-issued English transcript, university certification or CSSD verification.

The evidence and format can vary by notary office, document and intended use. The Guangzhou Justice Bureau’s academic-document notarization guidance is one official local example, not a nationwide checklist.

Does the Chinese Original Need to Accompany the Translation?

Many recipients require the source-language academic record and its translation together. The translation helps the reader understand the original; it does not independently prove that the university issued the underlying record.

There is no single worldwide rule governing whether the original, certified copy, scan or official electronic record must accompany the translation. Confirm whether the Chinese transcript is required, whether a copy is accepted, whether the university must deliver it, whether the applicant may upload the translation, and whether both documents must be submitted together.

For WES, the source-language document and requested translation have distinct roles. The applicant’s current WES Required Documents instructions control the documents and submission sources.

Sealed Transcripts, Translation and Direct Delivery

A sealed transcript is normally placed in an institution-controlled envelope using the university’s required closure, stamp or signature method. Sealing concerns custody and delivery; translation concerns language.

Confirm separately whether the university must seal or directly send the transcript, whether an approved electronic channel is available, whether the applicant may upload the translation, and whether the translation must be inside the same envelope.

A translation does not necessarily need to be placed inside the university’s sealed envelope. WES explains in its sealed-envelope guidance that required academic records must follow the prescribed institutional route, while requested translations may generally be uploaded through the applicant’s account.

That does not mean applicants may upload every underlying academic document themselves. WES also recognizes approved forms of electronic academic-record delivery, but availability depends on the institution and current instructions.

CSSD Transcript Verification Is a Separate Process

CSSD’s official English report name is the Verification Report of China Higher Education Student’s Academic Transcript.

The university or archive supplies the underlying academic record. CSSD performs the applicable verification process and issues a separate report. Its transcript-verification application guidance, supporting-material instructions and verification-report introduction explain the official framework.

Chinese and English report options may be available under the applicable process, but an English CSSD report is not a university-issued English transcript. CSSD does not recreate missing grades and is not a general translation agency.

Possible workflow: University transcript → professional translation where required → CSSD verification where required → recipient review. This is not a universal sequence.

The recipient may request the transcript, translation, CSSD report or a combination. For more detail, see CHSI and CSSD transcript verification.

Chinese Transcript Translation for WES

WES states that most cases may not require translation, but applicants must check the instructions for their current evaluation.

Where a translation is requested, documents for a U.S. evaluation outside English require an applicable English translation, while documents for a Canadian evaluation outside English or French require the applicable translation. The translation must be exact, word-for-word, clear and legible; applicant-prepared, handwritten and incomplete translations are unacceptable. The translator should work from official documentation.

Requested translations may generally be uploaded through the applicant’s account and do not generally need to be inside the institution’s sealed envelope. The underlying academic record must still follow its required official submission route.

Important: If WES specifically requests a translation, follow the translation instructions in the applicant’s current account. Do not assume that a university-issued English or French version can replace the independently translated version of the original documentation.

CSSD publishes separate CSSD–WES application and submission instructions and digital-transmission guidance. Not every applicant needs the same reports or delivery route.

Global Attest’s China academic document support for WES covers practical document assistance, while the step-by-step WES guide explains the broader evaluation process. If the problem is institutional delivery rather than translation, see university transcript delivery problems for WES.

Notarization, Apostille and Legalization

Not every Chinese transcript translation requires notarization or Apostille. These steps are relevant only when required by the recipient or applicable authentication route.

China’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs guidance indicates that education documents such as transcripts generally enter the appropriate notarization process before mainland-China Apostille or consular legalization. Convention destinations may use an Apostille where required; non-Convention destinations may require consular legalization.

An Apostille does not verify grades, certify academic equivalency, independently certify translation accuracy, replace CSSD verification, replace WES evaluation or guarantee recipient acceptance.

Confirm the required document and destination before proceeding. The full process is covered in China academic transcript Apostille requirements and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs’ official Apostille and legalization guidance.

Step-by-Step: Preparing a Chinese Transcript in English

  1. Identify the source academic record. Determine whether you hold an official transcript, archive record, electronic transcript, copy or unofficial grade report.
  2. Check the university’s language options. Ask whether an English or bilingual transcript is available for your program and cohort.
  3. Obtain the recipient’s written requirements. Confirm the accepted language, issuing source, translation standard and delivery method.
  4. Determine whether an independent translation is required. Do not assume a university English version automatically satisfies a separate translation instruction.
  5. Confirm whether the Chinese record must accompany it. Establish whether the recipient needs the source document, a copy or direct institutional delivery.
  6. Check translator requirements. Ask whether a declaration, credentials, signature, contact details or company certification is required.
  7. Separate delivery from translation. Confirm sealing, applicant upload, university delivery and electronic transmission independently.
  8. Check CSSD requirements. Obtain the applicable verification report only when required.
  9. Check notarization or authentication requirements. Use notarization, Apostille or legalization only where required.
  10. Send each item through the correct route. The transcript, translation and verification report may have different permitted senders.

If the case involves broader uncertainty about the academic record itself, China academic-record verification support may help identify the appropriate route.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Can I translate my Chinese university transcript into English?

Yes, an official Chinese transcript can generally be professionally translated, but the receiving organization determines who may translate it, what certification is required and whether the Chinese record must accompany it.

2. Does the translation need to be issued by the university?

Not always. Some recipients accept an independent professional translation, while others require a university-issued English transcript or another specified format. Obtain the recipient’s instructions first.

3. Is a university-issued English transcript better than a translated Chinese transcript?

Neither is universally “better.” A university English transcript and an independent translation are different document types. The correct choice depends on what the recipient requires.

4. Can I translate the transcript myself?

A personal translation may be useful for reference, but many formal recipients require professional translation. WES does not accept applicant-prepared translations when it requests a translation.

5. Does WES accept translated Chinese transcripts?

WES provides a route for professionally prepared translations when translation is requested, subject to the applicant’s current Required Documents instructions. The underlying academic record must still follow its required submission route.

6. Does WES require the Chinese original?

WES may require the original-language academic documentation in addition to a requested translation. Check the individual Required Documents instructions rather than relying on a general assumption.

7. Does the translation need to be sealed?

Not necessarily. Translation and sealed institutional delivery are separate requirements. For WES, requested translations may generally be uploaded and do not usually need to be inside the university’s sealed envelope.

8. Is a certified translation the same as a notarized translation?

No. Certification generally concerns the translator or provider’s statement about the translation. Notarization is a separate notarial process concerning a defined document, copy, signature or translation matter.

9. Does CSSD translate or verify transcripts?

CSSD performs the applicable transcript-verification process and issues a separate verification report. It does not issue the university’s underlying transcript or operate as a general translation agency.

10. Is a CSSD English report the same as an English transcript?

No. An English CSSD verification report records a verification result. A university-issued English transcript is the university’s academic record in English.

11. Does a Chinese transcript translation need notarization?

Only when required by the recipient or a later authentication process. Translation alone does not create a universal notarization requirement.

12. Can a translated transcript be Apostilled?

Not automatically as an ordinary private translation. For use from mainland China, the practical route generally requires an appropriate public or notarial document before Apostille. Confirm the document format and destination requirements first.
