Overview

Do You Need a China PCC for New Zealand?

Whether you need Chinese police clearance depends on your visa category, age, citizenship, residence history and Immigration New Zealand (INZ) instructions. Residence and temporary visa applications use different character-check rules.

Use INZ’s police certificate guidance together with the requirements of your visa and any individual request. A partner supporting your application may have separate obligations. Not every work, student, visitor or family application needs the same documents.

A China PCC concerns records held by Chinese authorities; it is not a New Zealand criminal record check. For assistance with China-side procedures, see our China Police Certificate Service. Global Attest is independent of INZ and Chinese issuing authorities. We provide document assistance, not visa decisions or regulated immigration advice.

Document Preparation

Required Documents: Start With Your China Records

This is a preparation guide, not a nationwide mandatory checklist. Requirements depend on nationality, city, residence period and the local authority’s review.

Identity & Travel History

  • Current passport bio page.
  • Former passport used during your stay in China, if available.
  • Chinese visas, entry/exit stamps and residence permits, as applicable.
  • Evidence explaining a name change or passport-number mismatch.

Residence & Authorization

  • Temporary Residence Registration Form, where applicable.
  • Dates and addresses in each Chinese city.
  • Work or school records that may help locate archives.
  • Power of attorney or other authorization, where permitted.
  • Photos or fingerprints only if the local procedure requests them.

The underlying police-issued record, a notarial certificate and a translation are separate documents. Chinese citizens may also need identity, hukou and local records. Tell us the exact INZ request and intended submission date so we can check the local route and required document version together.

Applying From Overseas

Send your former Chinese cities, dates, current location, passport history and available records. We assess whether authorization or a representative can be used under the current local procedure. Some steps may require personal attendance; remote processing is not available everywhere.

Missing registration, old passports or residence records need individual review. Replacement or supplementary records may be requested locally and are not guaranteed substitutes. Allow time for identity checks, archive searches, authorization and international delivery. See applying after leaving China.

China Document Format, Translation & Authentication

INZ’s published China Skilled Migrant residence checklist lists three items: a notarized police certificate, a copy of the original police clearance letter from the local PSB or police unit, and an English translation of that letter. It excludes letters from work units, their security departments, street offices or talent centres.

That checklist is specific to its stated application context. Confirm the current China entry in INZ’s country tool and your personalized document list before ordering. Do not assume that a notarial certificate alone replaces the underlying police record, or that this published checklist establishes every current visa route’s requirements.

INZ requires certified English translations of non-English police certificates, including certificates showing no convictions. Supply the source document too. Translation must be independent, accurately certified, and stamped or signed by the translator or business; applicants, family members and the adviser on the application cannot translate it.

Notarization and an apostille are different steps. An apostille concerns a document’s origin, not its underlying facts or visa eligibility. It is not an automatic requirement for every New Zealand application. Confirm authentication separately. See PCC format samples and document legalization services.

Check the receiving institution’s instructions before arranging an apostille or legalization.
Issue date & submission
A newly required certificate normally must be less than six months old at lodgement; previous-submission exceptions depend on the route.
Possible delays
Missing records, several cities, identity differences, local checks, authorization, holidays and international courier.

Check INZ instruction A5.10 before relying on a certificate from an earlier application. INZ can request a replacement or further evidence. An issue-date requirement is not a promise that Chinese authorities will finish by your filing date.

INZ’s July 2026 notice says required police certificates must be supplied upfront for student and other temporary applications. A receipt or confirmation of an application for a China certificate is not a routine substitute. Do not lodge an incomplete file on an assumption that INZ will wait.

We do not guarantee issuance, a fixed completion date, acceptance or a visa outcome. Translation, notarization, authentication and courier delivery may add time.

Your Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a China Police Certificate for a New Zealand visa?

It depends on your route. INZ instruction A5.5 normally requires first-time residence applicants aged 17 or over to provide certificates from their citizenship country and other countries where they lived for a total of 12 months or more in the last 10 years. Temporary visa rules generally use a cumulative New Zealand stay of at least 24 months, with certificates from citizenship countries and countries where you lived for five or more years since age 17. Check the category, exceptions and any officer request.

Does every New Zealand visa require overseas police clearance?

No. Visa type, age, residence history, previous certificates and specific exceptions matter. An officer can request a certificate where needed, and some categories have their own rules. A supporting partner’s character requirements may differ from the applicant’s. Do not apply another person’s checklist automatically to your own file.

Can I obtain a China PCC after leaving China?

An application may be possible after departure, depending on the former Chinese city, records and current authorization rules. We help assess the route, prepare documents and coordinate a representative where permitted. Some steps may require personal attendance, so a completely remote application cannot be promised for every case.

What documents are required?

A local review commonly starts with your current and previous passports, Chinese visas, residence permits where applicable, temporary residence registration, address history and work or study records. Authorization, photos or fingerprints may be requested locally. Also provide your INZ checklist. Confirm the requested source certificate, any notarial version and translation separately; there is no nationwide fixed checklist for every applicant.

Can I apply without a Chinese residence permit?

A visitor visa and a residence permit are different documents. Tell us your nationality, visa type, cities and dates so that the responsible Chinese authority can assess the available route. An INZ request does not compel local issuance. Alternative evidence may help a review but is not automatically accepted.

What if I have lost my previous passport?

Provide the former passport number, any surviving copies, Chinese visa records, registration information and relevant employment or school records. These may help connect historical records to your current identity. They are not guaranteed substitutes; missing evidence can delay or prevent issuance. Assess the case before relying on a deadline.

Does Immigration New Zealand require an English translation?

Yes, a non-English police certificate must have a certified English translation, even if it shows no convictions. Provide the source document and a correct translation stamped or signed by the translator or translation business. You, a family member or the immigration adviser assisting your application cannot prepare the translation. Follow your specific document-submission instructions.

Do I need notarization or an apostille?

Check the China-specific document format for your route. INZ’s published China Skilled Migrant residence checklist includes a notarial certificate, the underlying police letter copy and its English translation; do not treat that checklist as a universal rule for all visas. An apostille is a separate origin-authentication step and is not automatically required for every New Zealand application.

How long does it take to obtain a China PCC?

There is no dependable nationwide completion time. The city, available records, identity checks, authorization and authority workload affect timing. Translation, notarization, authentication and delivery can add time. We provide an estimate after review, without guaranteeing a fixed completion date, issuance or acceptance.

What if the Chinese authorities cannot issue my certificate?

Keep evidence of your attempts and any written response, and seek INZ instructions before filing. Under A5.10.1, a properly delegated officer decides whether a certificate is unavailable or unduly difficult to obtain and whether further evidence or a statutory declaration is required. Cost or delay alone is not sufficient. An explanation or application receipt is not an automatic waiver.
