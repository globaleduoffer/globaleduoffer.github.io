China Police Certificate With an Old or Expired Passport

An expired, replaced or lost passport does not automatically prevent a former foreign resident from applying for a China Police Certificate, but historical passport details may be important when matching China residence records.

By Global Attest Document Services Team · Published: August 16, 2026
Important: Old-passport requirements are case-specific. The useful evidence and accepted document form can depend on the city, residence period, visa history, locally available records, identity consistency and local-authority requirements.

Having an expired or replaced passport does not automatically prevent a former foreign resident from applying for a China Police Certificate. It is normal for an applicant to hold a new passport by the time a certificate is needed for immigration, employment, licensing or another overseas purpose.

However, if the applicant lived in China using an earlier passport, historical visas, residence permits, temporary residence registration and employment or study records may still be connected to the old passport number. The practical task is to link those historical identity records to the applicant’s current passport clearly enough for the relevant route and available local records.

Can I Get a China Police Certificate With an Expired Passport?

An old passport’s expiry date is not, by itself, a reason to conclude that a China PCC cannot be obtained. The certificate concerns a past period in China, so a passport that has since expired may still contain useful identity and immigration information from that period.

What matters is whether the applicant’s identity, residence period and passport history can be established using the evidence available for the relevant city. The route may differ according to local records and current local-authority requirements.

Why Does My Old Passport Matter for a China PCC?

An old passport can help connect several parts of a former resident’s China history:

  • The passport number used in local records.
  • Chinese visa labels or visa details.
  • Residence permits issued during the stay.
  • Entry, visa or residence information associated with that identity, where available.
  • Temporary residence registration completed at the time.
  • Employment or study records showing the same passport details.

A China Police Certificate relates to historical residence, not only to the passport an applicant holds today. For that reason, older identity information may help locate or match the records for the requested period.

What If I Have a New Passport Now?

Using a new passport is common. A clear case file may need to show the sequence:

Current Passport → Previous Passport → China Residence Period

Applicants may prepare the current passport identity page, the old passport identity page if available, old Chinese visas, residence permits and other records that connect both identities. Requirements are not identical in every city, so this is preparation guidance rather than a universal list.

The broader China Police Certificate document requirements guide explains which passport, visa, residence and authorization materials may be requested in different circumstances.

What If My Old Passport Is Lost?

Losing an old passport does not automatically mean that a China PCC is impossible. It does mean that the applicant may need to identify other records that show the old passport number, identity and connection to the requested residence period.

Potential evidence can include an old passport scan, old visa copy, residence-permit copy, temporary residence registration, employment records, study records or other historical documents. These materials may help with review, but no particular alternative can be promised as sufficient for every city or authority.

If records remain unavailable after reasonable checks, review what to do when a China Police Certificate cannot be issued for cautious next steps and possible supporting evidence.

Can I Use a Copy or Scan of My Old Passport?

A clear copy or scan may be useful for identifying the old passport number, confirming the spelling and order of the applicant’s name, matching visa or residence-permit records and building a reliable residence timeline.

A scan should not automatically be treated as a substitute for the original passport. Whether a copy is useful or acceptable depends on the purpose of the review, the other evidence available, the local route and the document form requested by the responsible authority.

What If My Passport Number Changed?

A passport number normally changes when a passport is renewed or replaced. That change is not the same as an identity error and does not automatically prevent issuance of a China PCC.

The goal is to establish a reliable connection between the old passport number, the current passport and the same applicant. Passport copies, old visas, residence permits and supporting identity documents may help build that connection, depending on the case.

What If My Name Is Different on My Old and New Passport?

A surname may change, spelling may be corrected, name order may be rearranged or transliteration may differ between passports. These variations should be documented clearly so that the historical records are not mistaken for records belonging to another person.

This article remains focused on old and replaced passports. For a fuller discussion of identity differences, see the guide to name, passport and residence record mismatches.

What If My Old Passport Contains My China Visa?

An old Chinese visa can help identify the passport used during a particular part of the applicant’s China history. It may also help organize visa type, validity dates and related residence-permit records.

Visa validity is not necessarily the same as the applicant’s actual residence period. A visa alone should not be described as proof of every date lived in China; it should be reviewed together with residence, registration, entry-and-exit, employment or study information where relevant.

What If My Residence Registration Uses My Old Passport Number?

A Temporary Residence Registration record may still display the passport number that was valid when the registration was completed. That does not necessarily indicate an error. It can be an accurate historical record of the identity document used at that time.

The current and previous passports can be organized alongside the registration record to explain the sequence. The guide to temporary residence registration for China PCC applications explains why these accommodation records may matter and what to consider when they are missing.

What If I Used More Than One Passport While Living in China?

Long-term residents may have used several passports across different parts of their China residence history. A simple timeline can prevent confusion:

  • Passport A: used from 2018 to 2020.
  • Passport B: used from 2020 to 2022.
  • Current Passport C: issued after leaving China.

For each period, list the city, residence dates, visa or residence permit and registration records connected to that passport. When passport changes overlap with different locations, the guide to multiple Chinese cities and residence periods can help structure the review.

Can I Apply From Overseas With an Expired Old Passport?

Many former foreign residents are both outside China and holding an expired previous passport. These circumstances can exist together without automatically preventing an application.

Remote feasibility depends on the city, historical records, passport evidence, authorization requirements and locally accepted route. The guide to obtaining a China Police Certificate after leaving China explains remote representation in more detail, while the China Police Certificate service page outlines the information needed for a preliminary case review.

Old Passport Checklist for China PCC Applications

If available, prepare:

  • Current passport.
  • Previous passport or passports.
  • Previous passport number(s), if known.
  • Old passport scans.
  • Chinese visa copies.
  • Residence-permit copies.
  • Temporary Residence Registration.
  • Employment or study records.
  • Documents showing a name change, if relevant.

Also prepare:

  • Chinese city or cities where you lived.
  • Residence dates for each location.
  • The passport used during each period.
  • Destination country and receiving authority.

This is a preparation checklist, not a nationwide government checklist. A case may require fewer, different or additional materials after the city and record history are reviewed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a China Police Certificate if my old passport expired?

Potentially, yes. Expiry alone does not prevent an application, but the old passport details may still be important for matching historical China records.

Can I apply if I lost my old passport?

A lost passport does not automatically make the case impossible. Other passport copies, visas, residence permits, registration records or historical documents may need to be reviewed, without any guarantee that a particular substitute will be accepted.

Is a scan of my old passport enough?

A scan may help identify the passport number and connect records, but it cannot be assumed to replace the original in every case. Accepted evidence depends on the local route and available supporting documents.

What if my passport number changed?

A new number after renewal or replacement is normal. The application materials may need to connect the old and current passports to the same applicant and residence history.

What if my name changed after I left China?

Prepare documents that explain the name change or spelling difference. The evidence needed depends on the type of change and the identity records available.

Can I apply from overseas using my current passport?

Remote processing may be possible, but the current passport may need to be reviewed together with earlier passport and residence records. Feasibility depends on the city and case history.
