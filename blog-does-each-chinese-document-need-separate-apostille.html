Does Each Chinese Document Need a Separate Apostille?

Determine whether your papers are separate public documents, independent copies or one qualifying multi-page or notarial document before arranging Apostille.

By Global Attest Document Services Team · Published: August 29, 2026
Direct answer: Not necessarily. Separate Chinese public or notarial documents—especially documents carrying signatures, seals or stamps of different officials or authorities—will normally need separate Apostilles for each formal origin that must be authenticated. A single multi-page public/notarial document or a single notarial certificate with its integral supporting pages may require only one Apostille.

HCCH guidance also notes that some competent authorities accept same-authority bundles, but current China MFA guidance does not publish a general bundling rule. Confirm the resulting document structure, the competent Chinese authority’s practice and the overseas recipient’s required number of originals before applying.

Does Every Chinese Document Need Its Own Apostille?

An Apostille authenticates the formal origin of a qualifying public document. Under the HCCH Apostille Convention, the relevant elements include the authenticity of the signature, the capacity in which the signer acted and, where applicable, the identity of the seal or stamp.

The correct question is how many separate public or notarial documents—and how many official signatures, seals or stamps—must be authenticated. Documents executed by different officials normally require separate Apostilles, while a genuine multi-page document or one qualifying notarial certificate may be treated as a single document. For the general route, see China Apostille requirements and process.

First Decide Whether You Have One Document or Several

Before counting Apostilles, identify the documentary structure of the set.

Several Separate Public or Notarial Documents

Separate certificates normally retain their own formal origins. If Document A carries one official’s signature or seal and Document B carries another’s, both origins cannot ordinarily be authenticated through one Apostille. The issue is whether each paper is a distinct public/notarial document or part of another qualifying instrument.

Multiple Copies of the Same Document

Multiple original notarial certificates are independently issued originals, while certified or notarized copies carry their own certifications. When several originals or certified copies must function independently, plan for separate authentication unless the competent authority and recipients confirm another arrangement.

Before counting them, review how originals and certified or notarial copies differ, because each form may present a different public or official certification.

An ordinary photocopy does not inherit the original Apostille or become another independently Apostilled original.

One Multi-Page Document

A genuine multi-page public or notarial document does not normally require one Apostille per page. Its pages must form one qualifying document, and its integrity, sequence and required attachments must remain intact.

No universal rule should be assumed for every-page stamps, riding seals, ribbons, staples, pagination or binding. Those features may depend on the institution, notary, document type and competent authority. Stapling unrelated documents together does not create one instrument.

One Notarial Certificate With Supporting Pages

Where a Chinese notary issues one qualifying certificate that formally incorporates underlying documents, the Apostille may relate to that certificate. The notary controls what can be combined, and supporting evidence is not necessarily an integral certificate page.

The Apostille authenticates the notarial certificate’s formal origin. It does not independently prove the truth, legal effect or overseas acceptability of every underlying record. See the difference between Apostille and notarization.

Can One Apostille Cover a Bundle of Documents?

The HCCH Apostille Handbook notes that some competent authorities issue one Apostille for a bundle executed by the same official or authority. It also recognizes that a notarized bundle might result in one Apostille for the single notarial certificate. This is conditional, not a universal right.

Current China MFA Apostille guidance does not publish a general mainland-China rule allowing same-authority documents to share one Apostille. Availability must therefore be confirmed with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs or the relevant authorized local Foreign Affairs Office.

The same seal, issuing institution or document type does not automatically create bundle eligibility.

Different Officials vs the Same Official or Authority

Documents executed by different officials normally require separate Apostilles where both origins need authentication. Documents executed by the same official or authority may present a possible bundle question, but the competent authority decides whether it is available.

A different situation arises where one later notarial act formally concerns several underlying private documents. In that case, the Apostille authenticates the notarial act—not each private document’s origin separately.

Physical binding does not by itself establish that several documents constitute one public document.

Translations, Attachments and Allonges

A translation formally incorporated into a qualifying public or notarial document may form part of it. A separately certified translation or notarized translator declaration may create another public-document origin. A loose translation is not automatically covered merely because it is attached.

For the broader language and certification framework, see how translation fits into the China Apostille process. This page remains focused on whether a document, bundle or attachment constitutes a separate public document.

An integral page or exhibit identified by the official certification may form part of the instrument. Supporting evidence, an applicant-supplied enclosure or a document executed by another official may remain separate.

An allonge is a separate sheet on which the Apostille is placed and securely attached to the underlying public document. It does not combine unrelated documents or extend the Apostille to every nearby paper. Examples of common formats are available in our China Apostille samples.

Do not detach an Apostille, its allonge or integral certified pages from the document structure for which it was issued. An Apostille cannot validly be transferred to another document.

Academic, Personal and Company Examples

A Graduation Certificate, Degree Certificate and Academic Transcript are separate underlying records. Current China MFA guidance requires these listed educational records to undergo notarization before Apostille. The final count depends on the resulting foreign-related notarial certificates—not automatically on the number of academic records.

A Birth Certificate and Marriage Certificate also remain separate underlying records. Submitting them together or placing them in one envelope does not turn them into one public document.

A Business Licence, Articles of Association and board resolution may have different origins and legal status. Their Apostille count depends on the resulting qualifying public/notarial documents, not the number of corporate files. See preparing Chinese company documents.

Confirm the Required Number of Documents and Originals

Before starting, confirm four separate quantities:

  1. The underlying records requested by the recipient.
  2. The number of original or notarial documents to be prepared.
  3. The number of Apostilles required for the resulting public documents.
  4. The number of physical submission sets required by all recipients.

These numbers are not necessarily identical. One organization might accept a certified copy, while another retains the original. Simultaneous applications may therefore require separate authenticated sets.

Confirm the required records, original notarial certificates, Apostilles and recipient sets separately. If a completed set is later rejected or considered incomplete, review what to check if an Apostilled document is not accepted overseas.

The document structure may also affect cost. Fees depend on the accepted notarial/public-document structure and the relevant notarial, authority and service arrangements. Do not assume that bundling is available or will reduce fees.

Multi-Document Apostille Review Checklist

Before applying, check:

  • destination country, intended use and overseas receiving organization;
  • exact records requested and issuing authority for each document;
  • signatures, seals or stamps involved;
  • number of originals and recipient sets;
  • whether pages form one instrument or several;
  • certified-copy and notarial-certificate requirements;
  • translations, attachments and integral exhibits;
  • whether any proposed bundle has authority confirmation; and
  • deadline and document-retention requirements.

This is practical guidance, not a universal authority checklist.

Common Mistakes

  • Counting pages instead of qualifying public documents.
  • Assuming all originals can share one Apostille.
  • Treating an ordinary photocopy as independently Apostilled.
  • Assuming staples or binding create one document.
  • Assuming every translation or attachment is covered.
  • Relying on a same-authority bundle without confirmation.
  • Detaching an Apostille or integral pages.
  • Preparing too few original sets for multiple recipients.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does each Chinese document need a separate Apostille?

Separate public or notarial documents normally require separate Apostilles for each formal origin that must be authenticated. Multi-page instruments and single-certificate structures may be treated differently.

Can one Apostille cover several documents?

Potentially, where the documents form one qualifying notarial/public instrument or an accepted same-authority bundle. China-specific availability must be confirmed.

Does every page of a multi-page document need an Apostille?

No. One genuine multi-page public or notarial document does not normally require one Apostille per page. Document integrity and authority requirements still matter.

Can one Apostille cover a translation?

Potentially, if the translation is formally incorporated into the qualifying public or notarial document. A separately certified translation may require separate assessment.

Can one Apostille cover an attachment?

Only where the attachment forms part of the issued or certified document structure. Physical attachment alone is insufficient.

Can two copies of the same document use one Apostille?

Not as two independently authenticated originals. Each original or certified copy intended to function independently should be assessed separately.

Can a Degree Certificate and Academic Transcript share one Apostille?

Do not assume so. They are separate underlying records, and the resulting China-side notarial-document structure determines the Apostille count.

Can several company documents be bundled?

Possibly through an appropriate notarial or authority-accepted structure, but there is no universal entitlement to combine them.

Can documents signed by different officials share one Apostille?

Normally no. HCCH guidance indicates that a single Apostille should not authenticate multiple documents executed by different officials.

Does one Apostille guarantee acceptance of the entire document set?

No. An Apostille authenticates the applicable formal origin. It does not guarantee that the recipient considers the complete document set sufficient.
