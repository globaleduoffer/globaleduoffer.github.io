Apostille or Embassy Legalization: Which Route Do You Need?

Apostille and embassy legalization are different routes. Confirm the destination country and receiving authority's instructions before choosing one; notarization and translation may remain separate requirements.

Translation, Notarization and Authentication Support

Translation may be a separate requirement. The correct sequence depends on the document, destination and receiving authority, and none of the following terms is interchangeable.

Notarization

A notary may certify a document, copy, signature, statement or translation, depending on the document and procedure.

Authentication

Authentication generally confirms the signature, seal or authority of the preceding official or institution.

Apostille

A simplified authentication certificate used in applicable cases under the Hague Apostille Convention.

Embassy Legalization

May be required when the apostille route is not accepted or the receiving authority specifically requests legalization.

These procedures generally authenticate relevant signatures, seals or official capacity; they do not automatically certify the truth of the underlying content. Certified or notarized translation may still be required separately.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is China document legalization?

China document legalization is the preparation and authentication of a China-issued document for use abroad. Depending on the document and destination, the route may involve notarization, a certified copy, translation, apostille, government authentication, or embassy or consular legalization. These steps confirm relevant signatures, seals, or official capacity; they do not automatically confirm the truth of the document's content.

Do I need an apostille or embassy legalization?

The correct route depends on the destination country, document type, purpose, and receiving authority. An apostille is generally used when the destination accepts the Hague Apostille Convention route. Embassy or consular legalization may be required when apostille is not accepted or is not appropriate for the use. Confirm the recipient's current instructions before beginning.

Which Chinese documents can be legalized?

Personal, academic, and commercial documents may be eligible, including birth or marriage certificates, police certificates, degrees, transcripts, business licenses, powers of attorney, and company records. Eligibility and preparation vary by document type and issuing authority. Some originals cannot proceed directly and may first require notarization, a certified copy, supporting evidence, or translation.

Do documents need notarization before legalization?

Some documents need notarization or an acceptable notarized copy before apostille, authentication, or embassy legalization, while other eligible public documents may follow a different route. The sequence depends on the document, issuing authority, destination, and recipient's requirements. A review of the available document helps determine whether notarization is a necessary first step.

Is translation included in document legalization?

Translation is a separate requirement and is not automatically included in an apostille or legalization certificate. The receiving authority may request a certified or notarized translation in English or another language. Global Attest can coordinate translation support where required, but the language, format, timing, and translator certification must match the destination organization's instructions.

How long does China document legalization take?

Processing time varies by document type, location, required preparation, government or embassy review, and appointment availability. Prior notarization, translation, missing information, public holidays, and additional document requests may add time. International courier time is separate. After reviewing the case, Global Attest can provide an estimated schedule, but authority processing times and final completion remain outside our control.

Can document legalization be completed after I leave China?

In some cases, legalization can be coordinated after the document holder has left China through an authorized representative or document assistance process. Availability depends on the original document, issuing location, notarization requirements, local authority rules, and destination instructions. Some cases may require original documents, identification, or a power of attorney, and not every step can always be completed remotely.

Can Global Attest guarantee acceptance of the legalized document?

No. Global Attest can review available requirements, prepare a case-specific checklist, and coordinate relevant document steps, but it cannot control a government, embassy, consulate, or receiving organization's decision. Final acceptance is determined by the receiving authority. Applicants should confirm the latest document format, translation, validity, and submission requirements with that authority before proceeding.
