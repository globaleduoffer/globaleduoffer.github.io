What If Your China Police Certificate Has the Wrong Name, Passport Number or Dates?

Identify whether an apparent error belongs to the police inquiry, a notarial certificate, a translation or the receiving organization's requirements.

By Global Attest Document Services Team · Published: September 3, 2026
Direct answer: An apparent wrong name, passport number or date on a China Police Certificate does not automatically mean that the police made an error. First identify which document contains the disputed detail and compare it with the source records. The issue may be an actual criminal-record inquiry error, historical identity continuity, incomplete residence coverage, a notarial-certificate error, a translation error or an overseas recipient requirement. China’s national framework provides a written objection and re-examination route for disputed criminal-record inquiry results, but correction follows only when the responsible authority verifies an error.

Do not begin by asking every organization in the document chain to “correct the PCC.” A police-issued Certificate of No Criminal Record, a Chinese notarial certificate, a translation and an Apostille or legalization are separate documents. The correct next step depends on where the inaccurate or disputed information appears.

First Identify Which Document Appears Wrong

A China PCC package may contain several documents with different issuers and functions:

  • a public-security criminal-record inquiry result or Certificate of No Criminal Record;
  • a Chinese notarial certificate relating to no criminal record;
  • a translation incorporated into the notarization or prepared separately;
  • an Apostille or consular legalization attached later for overseas use.

Record the exact title, issuing body, name, passport number, date of birth, city, period and wording shown on each. If the police document is correct but the notarial certificate reproduces a name incorrectly, the police inquiry is not necessarily the document that needs attention. If only a standalone English translation is wrong, reopening the police inquiry may be unnecessary.

For a fuller document-chain explanation, see China Police Certificate and notarial certificate differences.

Apparent Discrepancy vs Verified Police Error

A difference is not automatically an error. A certificate may correctly show the passport used during the historical residence period even though the applicant now has a new passport. English spelling may differ because of name order, spacing or transliteration. A shorter date range may reflect the period the authority could verify rather than a typing mistake.

Start with two questions:

  • Does the Chinese document conflict with the underlying identity or residence evidence?
  • Or does it merely differ from what the overseas recipient expected?

The second question may call for identity evidence, broader coverage or clarification—not correction. The recipient’s objection is important evidence of what it wants, but it does not prove that the Chinese inquiry result is wrong. See responding when China police evidence is questioned overseas for the broader post-submission analysis.

China’s National Objection and Re-examination Framework

The national Ministry of Public Security criminal-record inquiry rules allow an applicant who disputes the inquiry result to submit a written objection to the original accepting authority. That authority re-examines the result and gives a written response.

If the re-examination confirms that a Certificate of No Criminal Record or another relevant inquiry document is erroneous, the issuing public-security organ or its superior should promptly withdraw the erroneous document and reissue the relevant document.

This is an objection and re-examination mechanism, not a promise that every requested amendment will be approved. It should not casually be described as an administrative appeal, and it is not the same as asking for a typographical correction without identifying whether the inquiry result itself is disputed.

The national rule states a standard 15-working-day period for the written re-examination response after the objection is accepted. That is not a guaranteed total time for correction, withdrawal, replacement, notarial handling, translation or renewed authentication. Local submission mechanics and any additional document steps require confirmation.

Wrong Name or Passport Number

Compare the disputed document with both the current passport and the passport used during the China residence period. Several situations require different responses:

  • Historical passport number: the number may be accurate for the period even though the passport has expired.
  • Identity continuity: a new passport, later name change, different name order or transliteration may require documents linking the records.
  • Actual printing or data error: the document may show a number, spelling or date of birth that does not match the accepted source evidence.

Do not assume that an old number must be replaced by the current number. The objective is to show whether the record correctly identifies the same person. Available evidence may include current and former passports, official name-change evidence, visas, residence permits, accommodation registrations or earlier application records, depending on the issue.

Use the dedicated guide on whether a name or passport difference is an actual error before characterizing the discrepancy as a police mistake.

Wrong Dates or Residence Period

A date difference requires diagnosis before correction. The possibilities include:

  • the issued document contains a factual or production error;
  • the original request used the wrong start or end date;
  • the authority certified only the period supported by records available to it;
  • part of the residence belongs to another city or jurisdiction;
  • an earlier period is linked to another passport;
  • the overseas recipient requests broader coverage than the Chinese document provides.

Missing dates do not by themselves prove that the inquiry result is erroneous. If the police document accurately records the available inquiry scope, the practical route may involve another inquiry, another jurisdiction, supplementary evidence or the recipient’s unavailable-document procedure rather than correction.

For that separate problem, review when a China PCC does not cover the required period.

Police Document vs Notarial Certificate Error

If the public-security document is correct but the notarial certificate contains the wrong name, number, date or translated wording, the issue may belong to the notarial institution rather than the police.

The PRC Notarization Law and the Ministry of Justice’s Notarial Procedure Rules establish mechanisms for reviewing notarial certificates. Depending on the problem, an institution may handle a clerical error, issue a correction, withdraw an incorrect certificate or take another legally available step. The applicable response depends on whether the defect is merely textual or affects the substance of the notarized matter.

Do not ask police to correct a notarial mistake unless the underlying police document is also disputed. Contact the issuing notarial institution and provide both the police evidence and notarial certificate for comparison.

What If Only the English Translation Is Wrong?

Compare the Chinese source text with the English wording line by line. If the source document is correct and only a separately prepared translation is inaccurate, the translation may need correction or replacement; a new police inquiry is not automatically required.

If the translation is incorporated into a Chinese notarial certificate, the issuing notarial institution may need to review it. If it was separately certified or independently prepared, the responsible translator or certifying body may need to act. The route depends on the form submitted.

Translation accuracy, certification form and recipient language requirements are separate issues. See China Police Certificate translation requirements for the broader framework.

If the Document Has Already Been Apostilled or Legalized

An Apostille authenticates the formal origin of a qualifying public document—normally the relevant signature, signatory capacity and seal or stamp. It does not correct the name, passport number, dates or substantive content of the underlying document. China’s MFA explanation of consular authentication and the HCCH Apostille Handbook support this formal-origin boundary.

Apostille and consular legalization are also different authentication routes. The current China MFA Consular Authentication Measures govern the legalization route where applicable.

An authentication attached to one document cannot safely be treated as transferable to a corrected or replacement document. If the recipient requires authentication, the corrected or replacement document should be assessed separately and may require a new Apostille or legalization.

For route selection, see Apostille or legalization for a China PCC.

What to Review Before Seeking Correction

Collect the full document chain and compare it with the best available source evidence. This is a review checklist, not a universal correction application list:

  • the issued police certificate or inquiry result;
  • the related notarial certificate;
  • every translation supplied to the recipient;
  • the Apostille or legalization, if used;
  • current and former passports;
  • China visas, residence permits and accommodation registrations;
  • a city-by-city residence and entry/exit timeline;
  • the original application form or receipt, if available;
  • documents showing the correct spelling, date or passport information;
  • the overseas recipient’s exact objection or request.

Mark the first document in the chain on which the disputed detail appears. That step often determines whether to contact public security, the notarial institution, the translator or the receiving organization.

A Safe Correction Decision Path

  1. Identify the document. Determine whether the disputed detail appears on the police result, notarial certificate, translation or recipient’s own record.
  2. Classify the issue. Separate an apparent China-side error from identity continuity, incomplete coverage, notarial wording, translation or a recipient requirement.
  3. Compare source evidence. Map names, passport numbers, cities and dates against the available historical records.
  4. Use the appropriate review route. If the public-security inquiry result itself appears wrong, ask the original accepting authority about written objection and re-examination.
  5. Keep notarial errors separate. If the police evidence is correct but the notarial certificate is wrong, contact the issuing notarial institution.
  6. Correct translation at the correct level. If only the translation is wrong, identify whether it is incorporated, certified separately or independent.
  7. Do not relabel coverage as error. If dates are absent because of another jurisdiction or unavailable records, assess additional coverage rather than assuming correction.
  8. Reassess authentication. If a corrected or replacement document will be used overseas, confirm its current Apostille or legalization requirement.

Applicants outside China should not assume that every authority accepts a representative for every objection or correction step. The national mechanism and the local filing mechanics are separate; personal attendance, original-document return, authorization form and collection rules remain jurisdiction- and case-dependent.

What Correction Can—and Cannot—Achieve

Where the responsible authority confirms an actual error, the relevant document may be withdrawn, corrected or reissued under the applicable framework. But re-examination does not guarantee correction, and correction does not guarantee that an overseas recipient will accept the result.

A recipient may still require broader dates, another document type, a particular translation, current authentication or supporting identity evidence. Conversely, if the Chinese document accurately records the historical passport or available period, the best response may be to explain identity continuity or provide supplementary evidence instead of changing accurate information.

Keep all authority correspondence, replacement documents and earlier versions. If the disputed document was already submitted overseas, follow the recipient’s response deadline and instructions while the China-side issue is being assessed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a China Police Certificate be corrected?

Potentially, if the responsible authority verifies that the inquiry result or issued document is erroneous. First establish which document contains the error and use the procedure applicable to that issuer.

What if my name is wrong on my China PCC?

Compare the spelling with the passport and evidence used for the application. A genuine incorrect entry may justify review, while a name-order or transliteration difference may instead require identity-continuity evidence.

What if the passport number is wrong?

Determine whether it is actually incorrect or is the valid historical passport number for the certified period. A printing or data error should be distinguished from an accurate old number.

Is an old passport number necessarily an error?

No. It may correctly identify the passport used during the applicant’s China residence. The current and old passports may need to be linked for the receiving organization.

What if the residence dates are wrong?

Compare the issued dates with the request and available records. The issue may be an actual error, a limited verified period, another jurisdiction or broader recipient expectations.

What if part of my China stay is missing?

Missing coverage is not automatically a correction case. Identify whether the period relates to another city, passport, residence episode or unavailable historical records.

How do I challenge an incorrect criminal-record inquiry result?

The national MPS framework permits a written objection to the original accepting authority, which re-examines the result and responds in writing. Local submission mechanics must be confirmed.

What if the notarial certificate is wrong but the police document is correct?

Contact the issuing notarial institution. The appropriate response depends on whether the problem is clerical, affects the notarized matter or appears only in an incorporated translation.

What if only the English translation is wrong?

The translation may need correction or replacement without reopening the police inquiry. The route depends on whether the translation is incorporated into the notarization, separately certified or independently prepared.

What if the incorrect document has already been Apostilled?

The Apostille does not correct substantive content. A corrected or replacement document should be assessed separately and may need new authentication for its intended use.
