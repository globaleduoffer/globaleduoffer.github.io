China Divorce Certificate vs Divorce Judgment Apostille: Which Document Do You Need?

Identify whether you have a Civil Affairs Divorce Certificate, court judgment, mediation statement or another divorce-related record before arranging Apostille for overseas use.

By Global Attest Document Services Team · Published: August 29, 2026
Direct answer: A Chinese Divorce Certificate and a court divorce judgment are different records and may require different preparation for overseas use. A Civil Affairs Divorce Certificate records completion of an administrative divorce registration, while a court judgment or legally effective mediation statement results from judicial proceedings. Whether your record may enter the Apostille route in its issued form, requires a court-certified or notarial form, or should be accompanied by a court certificate confirming legal effect depends on the record, the competent Chinese authority’s intake requirements and the overseas recipient’s instructions. An Apostille authenticates the formal origin of the qualifying public document; it does not itself establish the divorce’s legal effect or recognition overseas.

Does a Chinese Divorce Document Need an Apostille?

Not automatically. The overseas receiving authority decides whether an Apostille is required and which Chinese divorce record it will accept.

A foreign marriage registrar may ask for proof that a previous marriage ended, while an immigration authority, court or civil-status office may request a different document set. Some recipients may need only the Divorce Certificate. Others may request a complete court judgment, evidence that the judgment took legal effect, a translation or a certified document form.

Confirm the required document before beginning the China Apostille requirements and process. This helps avoid authenticating a Divorce Agreement when the recipient needs a Divorce Certificate, or preparing a judgment without evidence of legal effect when the recipient expressly requires it.

Which Chinese Divorce Document Do You Have?

Civil Affairs Divorce Certificate

A Divorce Certificate (离婚证) is issued by the marriage registration authority after an administrative divorce registration is completed.

Under China’s current Marriage Registration Regulation, the parties first sign a written Divorce Agreement and apply jointly for divorce registration. After the statutory process is completed and the authority approves the final application, it registers the divorce and issues the Divorce Certificate.

The certificate records completion of that administrative registration. If the original is unavailable, marriage-registration archives or an officially reissued certificate may be relevant. They do not automatically substitute for the original certificate overseas, so the recipient must confirm the acceptable record.

A Divorce Certificate is also different from a Marriage Certificate. For the separate marriage-document route, see China Marriage Certificate Apostille.

Divorce Agreement

A Divorce Agreement (离婚协议) is not a Divorce Certificate. It records what the parties agreed. It does not, by itself, establish that the Civil Affairs divorce registration was completed or replace the resulting Divorce Certificate.

The agreement may address the intention to divorce, arrangements for children, property division and debts. An overseas recipient may request it when those substantive arrangements are relevant, but that does not make it interchangeable with the status document recording completion of the divorce registration.

Court Divorce Judgment

A Civil Divorce Judgment (民事判决书) is a judicial record issued in court proceedings. It is not a Civil Affairs Divorce Certificate.

The judgment’s procedural status can matter overseas. Under the PRC Civil Procedure Law, a first-instance civil judgment may be subject to an appeal period, while a second-instance judgment is final. A recipient may therefore want evidence that the particular judgment has taken legal effect. That evidence is not automatically required in every case.

Civil Mediation Statement

A Civil Mediation Statement (民事调解书) is a court-issued judicial document resulting from court mediation. It is not a private Divorce Agreement and is not identical to a court judgment.

Its effectiveness mechanism differs from a judgment, so the receiving organization may request the mediation statement, proof of its legal effect, or both. The issued court record should be reviewed rather than relabelled as a Divorce Certificate.

Certificate of Legal Effect

A Certificate of Legal Effect (生效证明 / 法律文书生效证明) may also be described as a Certificate Confirming the Judicial Document Has Taken Legal Effect or, more briefly, an Effectiveness Certificate.

The Supreme People’s Court certificate form can identify a judgment, ruling or mediation statement and confirm the date on which that judicial document took legal effect. The SPC provides this certificate for relevant situations in which proof of a judicial document’s legal effect is needed, including certain overseas-use cases.

The issuing court may provide this certificate following a relevant request. It is not automatically required for every judgment, mediation statement, divorce case or Apostille application.

Divorce Certificate vs Court Judgment: Which Does the Recipient Need?

The correct document normally follows the way the marriage ended:

Administrative divorce through Civil AffairsDivorce CertificateDivorce Agreement, if substantive terms are requested
Judicial divorce by adjudicationCivil Divorce JudgmentCertificate of Legal Effect, if required
Judicial divorce through court mediationCivil Mediation StatementProof of legal effect, if required

This is a decision aid, not a universal overseas filing list. A recipient may ask for more than one record, an original or certified form, or a specific translation.

Where several records are requested, confirm whether separate divorce records need separate Apostilles. The number of underlying papers does not, by itself, determine the final number of qualifying public or notarial documents.

Does Notarization Come Before Apostille?

Not necessarily in every divorce-document case.

Current China MFA Apostille application instructions provide a general route involving qualifying Foreign-Related Notarial Certificates (涉外公证书) or other certifying documents, while recognizing that certain documents may qualify for direct handling. The guidance does not publish one divorce-specific sequence covering every Divorce Certificate, judgment and mediation statement.

Under the PRC Notarization Law and the MOJ Notarization Procedure Rules, notarization can be relevant to marital status, legal facts or documents, copy-original conformity, signatures, seals and related translations where appropriate. Availability does not establish universal necessity.

Notarization may be used or required depending on the divorce record, the available original or certified copy, the notarial matter and the Apostille authority’s intake rules. Current national guidance does not establish that every Chinese divorce document must first be notarized.

The accepted form should be confirmed with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs or the relevant authorized local Foreign Affairs Office. For the functional boundary, see the difference between Apostille and notarization.

Which Document Actually Receives the Apostille?

The Apostille (附加证明书) relates to the qualifying public document presented and accepted by the competent authority. Depending on the accepted route, that document could be:

  • an eligible issued public document;
  • an eligible court-issued or officially certified document;
  • an eligible Certificate of Legal Effect, where that court-issued document itself is required and accepted for Apostille;
  • a Foreign-Related Notarial Certificate concerning marital status, a copy or another accepted notarial matter.

If a notary issues a certificate confirming that a copy conforms to an original, the document proceeding to Apostille may be that notarial certificate. The Apostille does not transform the underlying photocopy into an independently authenticated original. Direct eligibility should never be assumed from the document’s name alone.

When Might Proof of Court-Document Effectiveness Be Needed?

Proof of legal effect may be useful when the judgment was issued at first instance, the document does not clearly show its procedural status, an overseas court or authority expressly requests confirmation of finality, or the recipient asks for both the judicial document and a separate court certificate.

The issuing court controls whether and how a Certificate of Legal Effect is issued. Its possible relevance should be checked early, but it should not be presented as a universal prerequisite.

Originals, Certified Copies, Notarial Copies and Scans

An original Divorce Certificate may serve as source evidence, but its direct Apostille eligibility must be confirmed.

A reissued Divorce Certificate may be available after the marriage registration authority verifies its archives. The recipient must still confirm whether it accepts the reissued document and in what authenticated form.

A court judgment or mediation statement is a judicial record. Its accepted issued or certified form and any legal-effect evidence may need review. A court-issued or court-certified copy may carry official certification, but such copies are not universally eligible for direct Apostille handling.

A notarial copy certificate follows a different structure: the notarial certificate confirms copy-original conformity and may become the qualifying public document. An ordinary photocopy has no independent official status merely because it reproduces an original. A scan can help with preliminary review but does not automatically qualify for Apostille.

Does the Divorce Document Need Translation?

English is not universally required. The required language and certification method depend on the overseas recipient, destination filing rules and document route.

A translation may be incorporated into a Foreign-Related Notarial Certificate. A separately certified translation may constitute another document with its own certification. A loose translation is not automatically authenticated merely because it is submitted with an Apostilled divorce record. Confirm translation requirements before deciding its format or timing.

What Does the Apostille Prove—and Not Prove?

Under the HCCH Apostille Convention and the accompanying HCCH Apostille Handbook, an Apostille authenticates formal-origin elements: the signature, the capacity in which the signer acted and, where applicable, the identity of the seal or stamp. China MFA’s explanation of Apostille applies the same boundary.

Authentication is not recognition. An Apostille does not establish substantive validity of the divorce under foreign law, recognition of the divorce in another country, judgment finality unless the relevant public document certifies that fact, accuracy of an unrelated translation, remarriage eligibility, immigration or citizenship acceptance, or sufficiency for foreign civil registration.

The receiving authority applies its own laws and filing requirements. If a correctly Apostilled document is rejected, review what to check if an Apostilled document is not accepted overseas.

Check the Overseas Receiving Authority First

Before preparing the document, ask the receiving authority:

  • Do you require proof that the marriage ended?
  • Do you need a Divorce Certificate or the complete court judgment?
  • Will you accept a Civil Mediation Statement?
  • Is a Certificate of Legal Effect required?
  • Do you need the Divorce Agreement?
  • Must the record be an original, court-certified copy or notarial certificate?
  • Is an Apostille required?
  • What translation language and certification are required?
  • How many original or certified sets are needed?

These are practical review questions, not a universal official checklist. China MFA’s destination-requirement guidance likewise advises applicants to confirm the destination, purpose and foreign requirements when preparing documents.

China Divorce Document Apostille Review Checklist

For an initial review, prepare:

  • the type of divorce record available;
  • issuing Civil Affairs authority or court;
  • issue date and case level, where relevant;
  • original, reissued, certified-copy, photocopy or scan status;
  • whether a Certificate of Legal Effect is available;
  • destination country, receiving organization and intended use;
  • exact recipient instructions;
  • translation requirements and required number of sets; and
  • deadline.

Avoid sending unnecessary sensitive personal information before the service scope and document form have been confirmed.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming a Divorce Agreement is the Divorce Certificate.
  • Treating a judgment and mediation statement as identical.
  • Assuming every judgment needs a Certificate of Legal Effect.
  • Assuming every original can receive an Apostille directly.
  • Assuming every divorce record must first be notarized.
  • Submitting a scan when an official or certified form is required.
  • Assuming an attached translation is automatically authenticated.
  • Obtaining an Apostille before confirming the required divorce record.
  • Expecting an Apostille to guarantee overseas recognition.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Chinese Divorce Certificate need an Apostille?

Only if the overseas receiving authority requires it. The accepted original, certified or notarial form should also be confirmed.

What is the difference between a Divorce Certificate and a divorce judgment?

A Divorce Certificate results from an administrative divorce registration. A divorce judgment is issued through judicial proceedings.

Is a court mediation statement proof of divorce?

A legally effective Civil Mediation Statement can document a judicial divorce resolution. The recipient may also request proof of its legal effect.

Is a Divorce Agreement sufficient for overseas use?

Not necessarily. It records the parties’ agreement but does not alone prove that administrative divorce registration was completed.

Must a Divorce Certificate be notarized before Apostille?

There is no published universal national rule requiring this in every case. Confirm the accepted form with the competent Apostille authority.

Can a Chinese divorce judgment receive an Apostille directly?

Possibly, but direct eligibility should not be assumed. The judgment’s issued or certified form and competent-authority intake rules must be checked.

Do I need proof that the judgment has taken legal effect?

Only where the recipient or applicable document route requires proof of legal effect. A Certificate of Legal Effect is not automatic in every case.

Can a certified copy be Apostilled?

It may be possible if the certification creates an eligible public document, but ordinary and certified copies must not be treated as equivalent.

Is translation always required?

No. Language and certification requirements depend on the destination and receiving organization.

Does an Apostille guarantee recognition of the divorce overseas?

No. The Apostille authenticates formal origin; the overseas authority separately decides legal recognition and document sufficiency.
