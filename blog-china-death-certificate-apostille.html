China Death Certificate Apostille: Which Death Record Do You Need?

Identify the Chinese medical, household, cremation, court or notarial record required for overseas use before arranging an Apostille.

By Global Attest Document Services Team · Published: August 30, 2026

If a person died in mainland China, the first challenge may be identifying the correct record for overseas use. China has medical, police, household-registration, funeral, judicial and notarial records that can relate to a death, but they do not all prove the same thing.

Direct answer: China does not use one interchangeable death record for every case. A normal death may be documented by a current Resident Medical Certificate of Death, while a police-issued record may apply to certain non-normal deaths; household-cancellation evidence, cremation proof and a court judgment declaring death serve different purposes. For overseas use, the receiving organization must identify which record—and whether an original, certified copy or foreign-related notarial certificate—it requires. Current China MFA guidance does not publish one death-specific rule making every original directly Apostille-eligible or requiring every record to be notarized. Confirm the accepted form with the competent Chinese authority. An Apostille authenticates formal origin, not inheritance rights or overseas acceptance.

Does a Chinese Death Certificate Need an Apostille?

An Apostille may be required when a Chinese death-related public document will be presented in another country covered by the Apostille Convention. The destination authority must still confirm whether it requires an Apostille and which death record it will accept.

A probate court, civil registry, insurer or pension administrator may ask for different evidence. One recipient might require a medical death certificate, while another asks for a foreign-related notarial certificate, proof of kinship or a court judgment.

This article focuses on choosing the record and document form. For the wider application route, see the China Apostille requirements and process.

What Does “Death Certificate” Mean in China?

“Death certificate” can refer loosely to several records. Before arranging notarization or Apostille, identify the Chinese title, issuer and purpose of the document you actually hold.

Resident Medical Certificate of Death

Since 1 July 2026, the current national medical form is the Resident Medical Certificate of Death (居民死亡医学证明). The earlier title 居民死亡医学证明（推断）书 is legacy terminology and should not be presented as the current form.

Under the National Health Commission’s current death-certificate framework, the certificate documents the death and medically recorded cause. It is also an important source record for domestic household-registration cancellation and funeral administration.

For a normal death in a medical institution or during emergency transport, the relevant medical institution may issue it. For a normal death at home or elsewhere, the appropriate township health centre or community health service centre may issue it following the required declaration and death-cause investigation. A death outside a hospital does not automatically mean that the record must come from police.

The Resident Medical Certificate of Death is not the only Chinese record connected with death. Nor is its original form automatically proven to be directly eligible for Apostille or sufficient for every overseas filing.

Public Security and Household-Registration Death Evidence

Public Security documentation may become relevant for certain non-normal deaths handled by police without medical treatment. A police record of that kind is distinct from the medical certificate issued for a normal death.

Public Security also handles cancellation of the deceased person’s household registration. Depending on local procedures, evidence may be described as 户口注销证明, 死亡户口注销证明 or 死亡户口注销凭证. “Household-Registration Cancellation Evidence” is a useful general English description, but there is no verified single certificate title used in every locality.

This evidence generally records that the person’s household registration was cancelled. It is not identical to the Resident Medical Certificate of Death and does not necessarily include medical findings about the cause of death.

Cremation Certificate or Proof of Cremation

A Cremation Certificate (火化证明) establishes that cremation was carried out. Under the current Funeral Administration Regulation, the funeral service institution issues cremation proof after completing the prescribed cremation process.

That record is not the same as a medical death certificate. It does not necessarily establish the medically recorded cause of death, and it cannot be assumed to replace medical, police, household or court evidence in every overseas application.

An overseas organization may nevertheless request cremation proof separately, depending on the purpose of the filing and the recipient’s requirements.

Court Judgment Declaring a Person Dead

A Court Judgment Declaring a Person Dead (宣告死亡判决书) results from judicial proceedings. It establishes the person’s legal status as deceased through the judicial declaration procedure under the PRC Civil Code and is fundamentally different from medical evidence of a physical death.

It is not a medical finding about cause of death. An overseas recipient may request the judgment itself, a court-certified form or evidence that the judgment has taken legal effect.

The PRC Civil Procedure Law treats declaration of death as a special proceeding. A separate Certificate of Legal Effect should not be described as necessary in every case, although the receiving organization or applicable document route may request court confirmation of legal effect. The Supreme People’s Court provides an official Certificate of Legal Effect form for relevant situations.

Foreign-Related Notarial Certificates for Death-Related Matters

A foreign-related notarial certificate concerning a death-related matter may be issued by a Chinese notary based on sufficient supporting evidence. Depending on the requested notarial matter, the act may concern death, conformity of a copy to its source, kinship with the deceased, a signature or seal, a court declaration of death, or an incorporated translation.

The PRC Notarization Law expressly includes death, kinship and copy conformity among matters notaries may handle. Current MOJ service guidance also distinguishes normal death, non-normal death and declared death as notarial matters.

Underlying death record ≠ foreign-related notarial certificate ≠ Apostille

The notary determines the appropriate notarial matter and supporting evidence. There is no single “notarial death certificate” format that can be promised for every case. See the difference between Apostille and notarization for the functions of these two steps.

Which Death Record Does the Overseas Recipient Need?

The correct record is determined principally by what the receiving organization wants to establish. Possible requests include the medical death certificate, police evidence, household-registration cancellation evidence, cremation proof, a court declaration, a foreign-related notarial certificate, proof of kinship, a certified copy, a translation, an Apostille or several separate records.

Before starting, ask the recipient:

  • Which Chinese death record is required?
  • Must it be an original, official extract, certified copy or notarial certificate?
  • If a court declared the person dead, is separate proof of legal effect required?
  • What language and translation certification are required?
  • How many original or certified sets are needed?
  • Is an Apostille required for this particular filing?

These questions may arise in probate, inheritance, estate administration, pension, insurance, immigration, citizenship, civil-registration or repatriation matters. They do not mean that every such case requires the same document or an Apostille.

If multiple death-related records must be submitted, review whether separate death records need separate Apostilles.

Does Notarization Come Before Apostille?

Notarization may be used or required depending on the death-related record and the form accepted for Apostille, but current national guidance does not establish one universal death-document notarization sequence.

China MFA’s Apostille application instructions recognize compliant foreign-related notarial certificates and other qualifying documents, while certain documents may enter the route directly. They do not publish a death-specific rule resolving every medical, police, household, cremation or court record.

Confirm the accepted original, certified or notarial form with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs or the relevant authorized local Foreign Affairs Office before applying.

Which Document Actually Receives the Apostille?

The document receiving the Apostille might be an eligible original public document, an officially certified copy, a court-certified judicial document, a certification attached to a copy or a foreign-related notarial certificate.

Direct eligibility of the particular issued or certified form must be confirmed with the competent Chinese authority.

Where the qualifying document is a notarial certificate, the Apostille authenticates the notary’s signature, official capacity and seal. It does not separately convert every underlying record into an independently Apostilled document.

Originals, Official Extracts, Certified Copies and Scans

An original death record may be important source evidence, but holding the original does not establish direct Apostille eligibility.

An official extract or reissued record may be available under the responsible authority’s procedures. Its acceptance overseas and relationship to the original must still be confirmed.

A certified or notarial copy contains its own official certification. In that case, the certification may become the public-document origin authenticated by the Apostille.

An ordinary photocopy is not automatically an official document. A scan may be useful for preliminary review, but it is not automatically Apostille-ready.

If the applicant already has an older notarial death certificate, separate guidance explains whether an older Chinese notarial certificate may still be Apostilled.

What About Electronic Death Certificates?

The 2026 NHC framework supports a native electronic Resident Medical Certificate of Death. National health guidance recognizes electronic and paper versions for specified domestic uses, including household-registration cancellation and cremation.

A native electronic death credential is not the same as a scan of a paper certificate. Although national health guidance recognizes the electronic credential for specified domestic uses, current official guidance reviewed does not confirm that electronic death certificates are directly eligible for China’s e-Apostille process.

China’s currently published e-Apostille pilot concerns specified CCPIT Certificates of Origin, not death certificates.

Native electronic credential ≠ scanned PDF ≠ e-Apostille

Does the Death Document Need Translation?

English is not universally required. The language and certification form depend on the overseas recipient, destination and document route.

Confirm the required language and certification form with the overseas recipient. Where a translation is incorporated into a foreign-related notarial certificate, its relationship to that certificate should be clear. A loose or applicant-supplied translation is not automatically authenticated by the Apostille on a separate document.

What Does the Apostille Prove—and Not Prove?

Under the HCCH Apostille Convention, an Apostille authenticates the formal origin of a qualifying public document, including the authenticity of the signature, the capacity in which the signer acted and the identity of the seal or stamp where applicable. It does not independently verify the substantive content of the document.

For a death-related record, an Apostille does not prove cause of death, every medical statement, kinship, inheritance or probate rights, pension or insurance entitlement, immigration or citizenship eligibility, the accuracy of an unrelated translation, or overseas acceptance.

The overseas authority decides whether the complete document set is legally sufficient. If problems arise after issuance, see what to check if an Apostilled document is not accepted overseas.

China Death Document Apostille Review Checklist

Before submitting the document, confirm:

  • the precise Chinese title of the record and who issued it;
  • whether it concerns medical death, non-normal death, household cancellation, cremation or judicially declared death;
  • whether all pages, signatures and seals are complete;
  • whether the recipient requires an original, extract, certified copy or notarial certificate;
  • whether proof of kinship is separately required;
  • whether a court document needs proof of legal effect;
  • the required translation language and certification;
  • whether the competent Chinese authority accepts the proposed form; and
  • how many original or certified sets are needed.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating every Chinese death-related record as the same document.
  • Assuming the medical death certificate is always the overseas filing document.
  • Treating cremation proof as a medical death certificate.
  • Confusing household cancellation with medical cause-of-death evidence.
  • Assuming every death record must first be notarized.
  • Assuming every original qualifies directly for Apostille.
  • Treating a scan as a native electronic original.
  • Assuming an electronic death certificate automatically qualifies for e-Apostille.
  • Applying before confirming the recipient’s required death record.
  • Expecting an Apostille to prove inheritance rights or guarantee acceptance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Chinese death certificate need an Apostille?

It may, if the receiving organization requires an Apostille and the destination is covered by the Convention. First confirm which Chinese death record and document form the recipient requires.

What is the Resident Medical Certificate of Death?

It is the current national medical record documenting death and the medically recorded cause. It is also used as an important source for domestic household cancellation and funeral administration.

Is household-registration cancellation evidence the same as a medical death certificate?

No. It generally records cancellation of the deceased’s household registration and does not necessarily contain the medical findings recorded in the medical certificate.

Can a cremation certificate be used as proof of death overseas?

Possibly, if the recipient specifically accepts or requests it. It proves cremation and should not be assumed to replace medical, police, household, court or notarial evidence.

What if a court declared the person dead?

Depending on the case, the recipient may require the Court Judgment Declaring the Person Dead, a court-certified form, proof of legal effect, a related notarial certificate, or a combination of these documents.

Do I need a Chinese notarial death certificate?

Not in every case. A foreign-related notarial certificate may be required or useful depending on the underlying record, Apostille-authority intake and recipient instructions.

Can the original death record be Apostilled directly?

Direct eligibility of the particular original or certified form must be confirmed with the competent Chinese authority. No universal death-document rule was found.

Can a certified or notarial copy be Apostilled?

Potentially. The official certification or notarial certificate may itself be the public document whose formal origin is authenticated. Authority and recipient acceptance must be confirmed.

Is translation always required?

No universal English-translation rule applies. The overseas recipient determines the required language and certification form.

Does an Apostille guarantee acceptance for probate, inheritance, pension or insurance purposes?

No. An Apostille authenticates formal origin. The relevant court, registry, pension authority or insurer decides whether the document proves the matters required for its proceeding.
