What Is a China No Criminal Record Certificate?

Certificate and Issuing Context

A China No Criminal Record Certificate is evidence that a person has no recorded criminal conviction during a relevant period of residence in China, based on the issuing authority's records. Overseas institutions may describe it as a China police clearance certificate, China criminal record certificate, Chinese police certificate, or Chinese criminal background check.

Quick Answer

A China PCC records the result of a criminal-record inquiry by the relevant public security authority. The document form, scope and overseas submission requirements should be checked for your case.

Different Names Used for a China Criminal Record Certificate

These terms often refer to the same overall requirement, but the document format requested by an immigration office, employer, university, or embassy may not be identical.

Who May Need a China No Criminal Record Certificate?

Immigration and PR

Canada PR, U.S. immigration, Australia migration, UK visas, EU residence and other destination-specific police certificate checks.

Employment and licensing

Employer background checks, work permit files, professional licensing, school employment and government compliance requests.

Study and official use

University admission, student visa applications, citizenship, long-term residence, or official overseas document packages.

Eligibility & Responsible Authority

Foreigners may be able to apply if they previously lived, studied, or worked in China and can provide enough records for the relevant city. Requirements vary by city, and the period of residence, passport used at the time, residence registration, and visa history may affect eligibility.

Applicants who already left China may still have a practical route in many cases through an authorized representative. Before preparing documents, confirm the Chinese city, the exact period of residence, the purpose of use, and whether the receiving authority needs a police record, a notarial certificate, translation, apostille, or consular legalization.

Article 5 of the national criminal-record query rules provides for queries by foreign nationals who have resided in China for 180 days or more, handled by the exit-entry administration of a public security authority at or above county level in the place of residence. Articles 4 and 6 address authorized applications, query forms and identity/authorization documents. Local receiving channels and implementation must still be confirmed.

The relevant authority must be able to assess the identity and residence period using records it accepts. A previous visit to China alone does not confirm eligibility, and an overseas applicant must confirm the applicable route before arranging authorization.

Quick Answer

Your residence history and available records matter. Eligibility and the accepted route must be confirmed with the responsible Chinese authority; neither issuance nor overseas acceptance can be guaranteed.

Required Documents

The exact documents may vary by city, but commonly requested materials include:

Current Passport

Used to identify you and check your current passport details.

Previous Passport(s)

May help link the passport number used during your China stay to your current identity.

China Visa or Residence Permit

May help establish the type and dates of your stay; provide records relevant to the period being checked.

Residence Registration Form

May help confirm where and when you stayed. Ask the local authority which records it accepts.

Power of Attorney

Where representation is accepted, confirm the authorization form and the representative’s identity documents.

Other Supporting Documents

A query application form and additional locally requested records, such as entry/exit, work or study documents.

Preparation checklist and missing-record considerations
  • Current passport and any old passport used during the China stay
  • Chinese visas, residence permits, work or study documents
  • Temporary residence registration records or accommodation registration evidence
  • Entry and exit records where available
  • Authorization letter if someone applies on your behalf
  • Application purpose, destination country, and requesting authority details
This is a preparation checklist, not a universal mandatory document list. The responsible authority determines the evidence and document format required for your case. Before signing or sending documents, confirm whether originals, ordinary scans or certified copies are needed. Missing records require individual review and are not automatically replaceable.

If an old passport, residence permit or registration record is missing, request a case-specific assessment. These records are not automatically interchangeable. For multiple cities or a former resident overseas, confirm which authority can check the relevant period and whether representation is accepted.

For a deeper checklist and missing-record questions, read the China PCC required documents guide. This page provides an overview, not a uniform list for every city.

  3. 3

    Apply in person or through an authorized representative where the responsible authority permits it.

  4. 4

    Receive the document if issued, then check the identity details and the period or scope shown.

  5. 5

    Arrange Translation or Notarization if Required

    Check the language, translator qualifications and notarial document form requested by the recipient.

  6. 6

    Arrange Apostille or Legalization if Required

    Confirm whether authentication is needed and which route applies to the destination and document.

This is a general authority-application and document-follow-up route, not Global Attest’s quotation or service-delivery process. Local procedures and the order of steps may differ. Translation, notarization, apostille and consular legalization are separate matters; they are not automatically required together.

Applying After Leaving China

If you are no longer in China, the most important questions are whether you still have the passport used during your China stay, whether your residence registration can be located, and whether the city accepts an authorized representative. For the detailed remote route, read our China Police Clearance after leaving China guide.

For old-passport, multiple-city, non-issuance, and other case-specific questions, use our China Police Certificate problem guides.

A residence permit or registration record may help establish the stay being checked. Whether a representative can apply, and how the authorization must be prepared, depend on the responsible authority. Confirm those points before arranging signatures, notarization, authentication or courier delivery.

Quick answer: the exact document name and format may differ by city and issuing authority. A police-issued certificate, a notarial certificate and an apostille perform different functions. Confirm which document the receiving institution requests and which evidence the relevant Chinese authority or notarial office requires. Notarization does not automatically replace unavailable police evidence.

For examples of the different document types, see the China Police Clearance Certificate samples. A notarial certificate does not automatically replace police evidence that cannot be obtained.

Translation, Apostille & Legalization

A receiving institution may require English or another language and may specify translator qualifications or a particular translation/notarial format. A translation changes the language of the document; notarization and authentication serve different purposes. Do not assume an English translation is sufficient for every destination.

The answer depends on the destination country, receiving institution, translation requirements, notarization requirements, and document authentication rules between China and the destination. Where the Apostille Convention is in effect between China and the destination, an apostille is generally the relevant authentication route; where the Convention does not apply, consular legalization may be required. Review China’s official introduction to consular legalization and apostilles and the current HCCH Apostille Convention status table, then confirm the destination and receiving institution's current requirements. These authentication steps do not guarantee acceptance by a university, immigration authority, employer, regulator, or other recipient.

Timing, Issue Date & Acceptance

Allow for the different stages of document preparation and overseas use rather than treating every application as one fixed timetable.

Preparing records
Locating old passports, visa history and residence records can affect when an application is ready.
Authority checks and issuance
The responsible authority applies the relevant rules. Additional investigation or missing evidence can affect the handling time; issuance is not guaranteed.
Translation, notarization and authentication
These may add time where requested and should be confirmed for the actual document and recipient.
International delivery
Courier time, where needed, is separate from authority processing and any document follow-up.
Issue date and acceptance
The receiving institution decides how recent the document must be and what period it should cover. There is no single acceptance period for every overseas use; check any wording on the individual certificate and the recipient’s instructions.

No company service estimate on this page should be read as a statutory government processing deadline. If a certificate is unavailable, confirm the reason and the receiving institution’s instructions; do not assume another document will automatically be accepted.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a China criminal record certificate the same as a police clearance certificate?

In many overseas application contexts, yes. The wording may appear as China Police Clearance Certificate, China Police Certificate, China No Criminal Record Certificate, China Criminal Record Certificate, or Certificate of No Criminal Convictions. The exact document route can differ by Chinese city and by the receiving authority.

Can I apply after leaving China?

In many cases a former foreign resident may apply remotely through an authorized representative, but eligibility depends on the city, residence history, passport records, and the type of certificate required.

Can someone apply on my behalf?

Representative applications may be possible where the responsible authority accepts them. Confirm the authorization requirements, representative’s identity documents and applicant records for the specific route before arranging signatures, certification or delivery.

Do I need my old passport?

Old passports are often important because Chinese authorities may need to verify the passport used during the period of residence, study, or employment in China.

How long does the process take?

Timing depends on the city, document completeness, notarization, translation, apostille, or consular legalization requirements. Standard cases are often faster than cases with missing old passports or unclear residence history.

Is notarization required?

For overseas immigration, employment, university, or official use, a notarial certificate or certified translation may be requested even if the underlying police record has already been issued.

Do I need an apostille?

Check the receiving institution’s instructions and the document form. An apostille may be relevant where the Convention applies between China and the destination; otherwise consular legalization may be needed. These are alternative authentication routes where required, not automatic steps for every application.

What if the police refuse to issue the certificate?

If the authority cannot issue the requested certificate, first establish the reason and the residence period affected. Review the local application route, available supporting records and the receiving institution’s instructions for unavailable police evidence. A notarial certificate is not automatically a substitute for missing police evidence, and issuance or overseas acceptance cannot be guaranteed.

Who can apply for a China No Criminal Record Certificate?

Foreign nationals may be eligible based on their residence history and the applicable criminal-record query rules. The responsible authority must assess the relevant city, period and supporting records; having visited China does not guarantee eligibility.

Are the requirements the same in all cities?

No. Local acceptance routes, supporting records and document formats can differ. Check the competent authority for your Chinese residence history before preparing an application.

How long is the certificate valid?

There is no single acceptance period for every overseas use. Follow the receiving institution’s issue-date and coverage requirements, and check any wording on the individual certificate. A date stated on one sample is not a nationwide rule.

Can Global Attest assist with the whole process?

For case-specific support, see our China Police Certificate service. Available assistance depends on the city and your records; issuing and receiving authorities decide issuance and acceptance.
