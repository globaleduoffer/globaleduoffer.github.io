Overview

Do You Need a China PCC for U.S. Immigration?

For consular immigrant-visa processing, use the State Department’s police-certificate chart together with its China-specific civil document instructions. Age, nationality, residence history and any arrest history affect which certificates are needed.

Do not apply the immigrant-visa checklist automatically to nonimmigrant visas or USCIS filings inside the United States. K visa cases, employers, licensing bodies and other recipients may issue their own requests. A China PCC is not an FBI Identity History Summary.

For China-side assistance, see our China Police Certificate Service. Global Attest is independent of USCIS, the Department of State, NVC, U.S. consular posts and Chinese authorities. We provide document assistance, not immigration legal advice or visa decisions.

Document Preparation

Required Documents: Start With Your China Records

This is a preparation guide, not a nationwide mandatory checklist. Requirements depend on nationality, city, residence period and the local authority’s review.

Identity & Travel History

  • Current passport bio page.
  • Former passport used during your stay in China, if available.
  • Chinese visas, entry/exit stamps and residence permits, as applicable.
  • Evidence explaining a name change or passport-number mismatch.

Residence & Authorization

  • Temporary Residence Registration Form, where applicable.
  • Dates and addresses in each Chinese city.
  • Work or school records that may help locate archives.
  • Power of attorney or other authorization, where permitted.
  • Photos or fingerprints only if the local procedure requests them.

Chinese citizens may also need identity, hukou and local records. Requirements vary by nationality, city and available archives. For U.S. immigrant-visa use, the China reciprocity schedule describes a local notary’s Police Certificate based on PSB or Entry & Exit records, with translation included. The underlying police-issued document is not the same final document.

Application Process

Step-by-Step: Getting a China PCC for USA

  1. 1. Prepare Documents

    Review your case instructions, China residence history and local application route.

  2. 2. Submit Application

    Apply through the competent PSB or Entry & Exit route; use a representative only where permitted.

  3. 3. Receive PCC

    Check the police-issued record and arrange the required notarial certificate for consular use.

  4. 4. Translation & Authentication (If Required)

    Complete the applicable notarial and translation format; add authentication only if requested.

  5. 5. Submit for U.S. Application

    Follow NVC, consular-post or other recipient instructions for your specific case.

Applying From Overseas

Tell us your former Chinese cities, dates, current location and available documents. Local police and notary procedures determine whether authorization, online steps or a representative can be used. Some steps may require personal attendance; remote processing is not available everywhere.

Confirm how documents can be collected and delivered, including any international courier arrangements. See applying after leaving China. A visa deadline does not remove Chinese identity or record checks.

Translation, Notarization & Authentication

For immigrant-visa interviews in Guangzhou, the post’s checklist requires Chinese-issued documents to be translated and notarized locally. Follow your own post’s instructions; a raw PSB certificate, a notarial police certificate and a standalone translation are not interchangeable.

For USCIS submissions, 8 CFR 103.2(b)(3) requires a complete English translation with the translator’s accuracy and competence certifications. This does not make every USCIS filing a police-certificate case.

An apostille authenticates origin-related matters, not the underlying facts or visa eligibility. It is not an automatic requirement for all U.S. applications. View PCC format samples and document legalization services.

Common Problems and Delays

  • Missing registration, old passports, visas or residence records.
  • Different names or passport numbers across historical records.
  • Incomplete dates, addresses, employment or school records.
  • Local restrictions on remote or representative applications.
  • Additional checks or gaps in the period covered by an issued certificate.

Your Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a China Police Certificate for a U.S. immigrant visa?

Use the State Department’s immigrant-visa police-certificate chart and China reciprocity instructions. The general chart starts at age 16 and distinguishes nationality, current residence, former residence and arrest history. Check the period you lived in China and your consular post’s checklist rather than assuming that one residence threshold applies to everyone.

Is a China PCC required for every U.S. visa?

No. Consular immigrant visas, nonimmigrant visas, K visa processing and USCIS applications inside the United States do not share one universal document checklist. Submit a China PCC when the applicable instructions or an individual request require it. USCIS form instructions and requests should be reviewed separately from NVC document collection.

Can I apply for a China PCC after leaving China?

A route may be available, depending on the city, identity evidence, archives and police and notary procedures. Authorization or a representative may be permitted, while some offices require personal attendance. We assist with preparation and coordination where allowed, without guaranteeing that every case can be completed remotely.

What documents do I need?

Prepare available current and previous passports, Chinese visas or residence permits, temporary residence registration, address history and work or study records. An authorization letter, photos, fingerprints or further local evidence may be requested. Your U.S. case notice helps identify the purpose and deadline. The final China-side checklist depends on the competent offices.

What if I no longer have my previous passport?

Request an individual review before ordering replacement records. Previous passport copies or numbers, visas, registration, work or school records and supporting explanations may help identify archives. They are not automatically accepted substitutes, and the relevant authority may still require evidence that cannot be recovered.

Can I apply without a Chinese residence permit?

A visitor visa and a residence permit are different documents. Ask the competent police and notary offices to assess your nationality, stay dates and surviving records. Do not assume either automatic eligibility or a nationwide exception. Local requirements and the availability of verifiable records determine whether the application can proceed.

Does the U.S. require an English translation?

For Chinese police certificates in immigrant-visa processing, the China reciprocity instructions specify translation included in the notarial document. Follow the receiving consular post’s format. For documents submitted to USCIS, a full English translation must be certified as complete and accurate by a competent translator under 8 CFR 103.2(b)(3). A translation alone does not replace a required notarial certificate.

Do I need notarization or an apostille?

For the Chinese police document used in U.S. immigrant-visa processing, follow the local notary format in the China reciprocity schedule and your consular post’s checklist. This does not mean every U.S. application requires notarization or an apostille. Apostille and consular legalization are distinct procedures; arrange extra authentication only when the actual recipient requires it.

How long does it take to obtain a China PCC?

There is no reliable nationwide completion time. The estimate depends on the city, records, identity checks and authority workload. Notarial review, translation, authorization and delivery can add time. We assess the practical route before giving an estimate and do not promise a fixed date or result.

What if the Chinese authorities cannot issue my certificate?

For NVC immigrant-visa document collection, distinguish a document listed as unavailable in the country schedule from a document you cannot obtain for another reason. Follow the written-explanation instructions and keep evidence of your attempts. A consular officer decides whether the document must still be obtained. USCIS cases follow their own evidence rules; a declaration is not an automatic replacement or waiver.
