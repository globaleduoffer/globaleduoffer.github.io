China Police Certificate Requirements: Documents Needed for Foreigners

The documents needed for a China Police Certificate can depend on the city, residence history, passport history and whether the applicant is applying in China or through an authorized representative from overseas.

By Global Attest Document Services Team · Published: August 14, 2026
Important: China Police Certificate requirements are not identical in every city. The documents below are common planning categories, not a nationwide list that guarantees issuance.

Foreign applicants often begin by asking for one fixed list of China PCC required documents. In practice, a local authority may need to connect the applicant’s identity, passport, visa status, address and residence period to records held for a particular city.

The appropriate document set can therefore vary according to where and when the applicant lived in China, which passport was used and how the application will be handled. Prepare the strongest available record set, then confirm the current local route before submitting originals or arranging special documents.

What Documents Are Usually Required for a China Police Certificate?

Commonly requested materials may include:

  • A clear copy of the current passport information page.
  • The previous passport used while living in China, where relevant.
  • Copies of China visas connected to the requested residence period.
  • Residence permit copies or other records of authorized stay.
  • A Temporary Residence Registration Form or other police accommodation registration record, where relevant.
  • A Power of Attorney when an authorized representative handles a remote application.
  • Employment, work-permit, university or study records where requested or useful for establishing the residence history.
  • Fingerprints where required by the responsible local authority.

This is a preparation guide rather than a universal checklist. Requirements vary by city, and an authority may request fewer, different or additional China police certificate documents after reviewing the individual history.

Do I Need My Old Passport?

A previous passport may be important when it was the passport used during the applicant’s stay in China. It can help connect the applicant’s identity, former passport number, visa history and residence period to historical records.

The old passport itself may not be the only available evidence. Copies of its information page, China visas, residence permits, work or study files and earlier immigration submissions may also show relevant details. If the passport has been lost or cannot be retrieved, do not assume that the PCC is automatically impossible. The local records and other supporting documents need to be reviewed first. For cases involving an old or expired passport, replacement, loss or a previous passport number, the dedicated guide explains how to organize the historical passport evidence.

Is Temporary Residence Registration Required?

Temporary residence registration can help connect a foreign resident to an address, city, passport and period of stay. It may be particularly useful where a local office needs to identify older accommodation or residence records.

Its role is not identical in every case. Some applicants retain a paper form, while others may have hotel, landlord, university or locally accessible records. The separate guide to Temporary Residence Registration for China PCC applications explains how to assess this record without treating it as a nationwide automatic requirement.

Do I Need a Power of Attorney If I Am Outside China?

A China PCC power of attorney may be needed when a representative is authorized to make enquiries, submit documents or collect a certificate for a former resident who is outside China.

Do not assume that one Power of Attorney template works everywhere. The responsible office or processing route may have its own wording, signature, identification, witness, notarization or legalization requirements. Confirm the accepted format before signing or sending an original.

Are Fingerprints Required?

Fingerprint requirements vary by city and local authority. Some application routes may request a fingerprint form or locally specified fingerprint process, while others may rely on different identity and residence records.

Applicants should not arrange fingerprints based only on another person’s case. First confirm whether fingerprints are required, which form or card is accepted, how the prints must be taken and whether any certification is needed.

What If I Lived in Multiple Chinese Cities?

Records and issuing procedures can be city-specific. An applicant who lived in more than one city may need to organize a separate residence period, passport history and supporting record set for each location.

A certificate or record connected to one city should not automatically be assumed to cover every other city. Use the guide to China Police Certificates for multiple cities and residence periods to build a city-by-city timeline before confirming which documents are needed.

What If My Name or Passport Details Changed?

Historical China records may show an old passport number, a different spelling or order of names, a missing middle name or identity details used before a legal name change. These differences should be recorded rather than silently corrected.

Current and previous passports, visas, residence permits and name-change records may help establish continuity. The guide to name, passport and residence record mismatches explains how to compare the versions and identify which differences may need clarification.

What If Some Documents Are Missing?

A missing old passport, residence registration form, visa copy or other supporting record does not always mean that a China PCC cannot be obtained. The effect of the gap depends on the city, residence history, records available locally and alternative documents that may help establish the same period or identity connection.

Start by identifying exactly what is missing and which city and dates are affected. Then review copies, emails, prior applications, employer or university files and other records before deciding that the route has failed. If the gap is already preventing progress, see what to do if a China Police Certificate cannot be issued.

Can I Apply After Leaving China?

Many former foreign residents can apply from overseas through an authorized representative, but the available route and supporting documents depend on the relevant local authority and the applicant’s records.

Before arranging a Power of Attorney or sending identity documents, review the city, years of residence, visa type, current and previous passport situation and destination-country requirements. The China Police Certificate service page explains the remote application process and how a preliminary feasibility review works.

China PCC Document Checklist

Usually prepare

  • Current passport information page.
  • Previous passport used in China, if relevant and available.
  • China visa and residence permit records connected to the requested period.
  • Temporary residence registration or other available accommodation records.

May also be required

  • A Power of Attorney for an authorized representative.
  • Employment, work-permit, university or study evidence.
  • Fingerprints in the format accepted by the relevant local authority.
  • Additional local supporting records for a particular city or historical period.

Check that names, passport numbers, cities and dates are consistent across the available records. Where they differ, note the reason and keep documents that help connect the versions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I apply without my old passport?

Possibly. An old passport may help connect identity, visas and residence periods, but a missing passport does not automatically make an application impossible. The city, locally available records and other supporting documents need to be assessed.

What if I lost my temporary residence registration?

A missing registration form does not produce the same result in every case. Identify the city, residence dates and passport used, then check what local records or other supporting documents remain available.

Do I need fingerprints?

Not in every case. Fingerprint requirements vary by city, local authority and application route, so confirm the relevant format before arranging fingerprints.

Do I need a Power of Attorney?

A Power of Attorney may be required when a representative handles an application in China. Its wording, signing and supporting-document requirements can vary by city and route.

Are China PCC requirements the same in every city?

No. Common document categories exist, but local records, procedures and requested supporting materials can differ by city, residence period and individual history.

Can I apply from overseas?

Many former foreign residents can use an authorized representative after leaving China, but feasibility and required documents depend on the relevant city and the records available for the applicant.
