How to Apostille a Chinese Employment or Resignation Certificate

Understand which employment-related document you have, when a qualifying notarial or official certification may be involved, and what the Apostille actually authenticates.

By Global Attest Document Services Team · Published: September 2, 2026
Direct Answer: An ordinary employment, resignation or work-experience document issued by a private Chinese employer is not automatically an Apostille-eligible public document. Depending on the document, notarial matter, competent authority and recipient instructions, a Chinese notarial act or another official certification may first create the qualifying public-document origin. The Apostille then authenticates that formal origin—not every employment fact stated in the employer record. There is no single nationwide employment-certificate checklist, so the document, supporting evidence, notarial route, destination and receiving organization should be reviewed before preparation.

Sources reviewed: 2 September 2026. Local notarial evidence requirements, representative arrangements, work-permit procedures and overseas recipient rules can change, so confirm current requirements before submission.

For an overview of the wider route, see China Apostille requirements and process.

What Counts as a Chinese Employment-Related Document?

“Employment certificate” can refer to several different Chinese documents:

  • 在职证明 usually confirms current employment.
  • 工作证明 is a broad label for proof of work or employment.
  • 任职证明 may confirm a position or appointment.
  • 离职证明 records that someone has left an employer, but does not necessarily prove voluntary resignation.
  • 解除劳动合同证明 concerns dissolution or termination of a labour contract.
  • 终止劳动合同证明 concerns expiration or termination of a labour contract.
  • 工作经历证明 is closer to a work-experience certificate or employment reference.
  • 劳动合同 is the employment contract itself.

These documents are not interchangeable. Their issuer, wording and legal function must be examined before deciding what should be notarized or Apostilled.

Employment Certificate vs Resignation or Termination Certificate

Current-employment evidence and end-of-employment evidence answer different questions.

Article 50 of China’s Labour Contract Law requires an employer to issue a certificate when a labour contract is dissolved or terminated. Article 24 of the Implementing Regulations says that certificate should state the contract term, ending date, position and years worked for the employer.

That statutory framework does not require the certificate to contain salary, detailed duties, working hours or a performance assessment. An overseas immigration authority, licensing body or employer may nevertheless request those additional details.

A termination notice is not automatically the statutory ending certificate, as the MOHRSS terminology response illustrates. A detailed work-experience reference is also not automatically the same document.

Work-Experience Letter, Labour Contract and Government Records

A 工作经历证明 may describe the employee’s position, dates, duties or projects. It is normally an employer-issued reference rather than the statutory certificate issued when employment ends.

A labour contract may show the agreed employer, position, term and remuneration. It does not, by itself, prove that the employee performed every stated duty throughout the full contract period.

Government records such as a Foreigner’s Work Permit, tax record or social-insurance record have still different functions. They may support employment history, but they do not automatically replace the particular document requested overseas.

Private Employer Document vs Public or Notarial Document

The HCCH Apostille Convention applies to public documents. An ordinary letter from a private Chinese company does not automatically become a Convention public document merely because it carries company letterhead, a company seal or an HR signature.

The HCCH Apostille Handbook, Second Edition distinguishes private documents from public notarial acts and official certificates placed on private documents.

A possible structure is:

Private employer evidence
→ legally defined notarial or official certification
→ qualifying public or notarial document
→ Apostille

This is not a universal mandatory sequence. Some documents may use another competent verification route, while a document that is officially eligible may receive different treatment.

The key question is: What public or official certification will the Apostille authenticate?

Applicants comparing the available forms should also confirm whether to use an original, certified copy or notarial certificate.

Can an Employment or Resignation Certificate Be Apostilled Directly?

Not automatically.

Under the China MFA guidance and current Q&A reviewed for this article, Chinese-mainland documents intended for Apostille or consular legalization generally need an eligible notarial or other qualifying verification document before submission, unless the particular document is eligible for direct processing.

The guidance does not identify every private employment or resignation letter as directly eligible. The competent authority must be able to recognize the signature, seal or official certification on the qualifying document. No universal employment-document route exists.

What Could a Chinese Notarial Certificate Certify?

Depending on the requested notarial matter and available evidence, a Chinese notarial certificate may concern:

  • A legally significant employment fact or work experience
  • The authenticity or legality of a document
  • A signature or seal
  • Conformity of a copy with an original
  • Another defined matter within the notarial institution’s competence

It is therefore too imprecise to say only that “the employment certificate is notarized.” The wording of the completed notarial certificate determines what the notarial act actually certifies.

Under the PRC Notarization Law, the applicant must provide truthful, lawful and sufficient evidence. The notarial institution may examine or verify the requested matter and require additional materials.

What Supporting Evidence May Be Reviewed?

Possible evidence includes:

  • An employer-issued certificate
  • The labour contract
  • Foreigner’s Work Permit records
  • A work-permit cancellation certificate
  • Individual income-tax records
  • Social-insurance contribution records
  • Payslips or payroll records
  • Bank records showing salary payments
  • Company-registration, name-change or dissolution records

This is a practical evidence list, not a nationwide government checklist. Different notarial matters and receiving organizations may require different combinations.

Local official Wuhan work-experience guidance and Hubei notarial evidence guidance show that employer certificates, contracts, tax records and social-insurance records may be considered when work experience is reviewed. They do not make any one record an automatic substitute for the employer document.

What If You Already Left China?

A former employee abroad may be able to authorize a representative to coordinate document collection, notarization and Apostille submission. Whether representation is accepted, what form of authorization is required and whether personal participation is necessary depend on the notarial matter and authorities involved.

The wider remote-processing considerations are covered in getting a China Apostille after leaving China.

If a representative requires formal authorization, confirm the China Apostille requirements for a Power of Attorney before signing it. There is no single POA format suitable for every authority.

What If the Former Employer Refuses, Closes or Cannot Be Contacted?

Where an employer should have issued the statutory dissolution or termination certificate, labour-law remedies may exist if it refuses. That does not mean the employer must provide:

  • Any overseas recipient’s preferred reference format
  • Detailed salary or duty wording
  • Unlimited duplicate copies
  • Expedited reissuance

If the company has dissolved or cannot be contacted, old employer documents, contracts, work-permit records, tax records, social-insurance records, payroll evidence and official company records may help establish the employment history.

Alternative records may assist the notarial or recipient review, but they do not automatically replace the requested employer certificate.

Foreigner’s Work Permit and Cancellation Records

A Foreigner’s Work Permit may support the person’s authorization to work and the employer or validity information recorded in the permit or official record.

It does not automatically prove:

  • Duties actually performed
  • Salary actually paid
  • Performance
  • Every period of actual employment
  • Complete employment history

An official cancellation certificate may support employment chronology, but it is not automatically equivalent to an employer-issued employment or separation document. Current local procedures, including Beijing’s published cancellation guidance, should be checked for the particular record.

Tax, Social-Insurance and Payroll Evidence

Tax records may corroborate reported taxable income, withholding and relevant periods. Social-insurance records may corroborate contribution periods. Payslips and bank records may provide further payment evidence.

Each has a limited evidentiary function. None conclusively proves every employment fact, and none automatically substitutes for the reference or certificate required by the receiving organization.

Does the Employment Document Need Translation?

The required language and certification form depend on the destination and recipient.

China MFA’s translation guidance directs attention to the language required for the destination. A translation incorporated into a Chinese foreign-related notarial instrument differs from an applicant-prepared loose translation. Terms such as 离职证明, 工作证明 and 工作经历证明 should be translated according to the actual document rather than assigned one generic English title.

See China Apostille translation requirements for the general timing and certification framework.

What Does the Overseas Recipient Actually Require?

Recipients can request very different employment evidence.

Canadian immigration guidance, for example, identifies contracts, employer references, pay records and tax documents as possible evidence. Certain programs request dates, duties, salary, benefits and working hours.

Australian skilled-visa guidance warns that an employment reference alone may be insufficient and may require supporting bank, payslip or tax evidence. Professional assessment bodies can impose their own requirements for duties, dates, hours, salary and referee contact details.

Consistent with China MFA’s destination guidance and foreign-related notarial guidance, the overseas recipient determines which employment facts it needs. Chinese employers, notarial institutions and authorities determine what document or certification they can legally issue.

What Does the Apostille Prove—and Not Prove?

For the qualifying public or notarial document, an Apostille authenticates:

  • The relevant signature
  • The capacity in which the signer acted
  • The seal or stamp, where applicable

Where the qualifying document is a notarial act or official certification connected to private employer evidence, the Apostille authenticates the formal origin of that notarial act or certification.

It does not independently prove employment dates, job title, duties, salary, hours, performance, current employment, professional competence, the truth of every employer statement or final overseas acceptance.

Apostille or Consular Legalization?

The destination and applicable Convention relationship must be checked. If the Apostille Convention applies between China and the destination, an eligible public or notarial document may use the Apostille route.

Where that Convention relationship does not apply, consular legalization may instead be required. Recipient instructions still determine whether the document itself is suitable.

China Employment Document Review Checklist

This is a practical review checklist, not a nationwide government checklist:

  • What is the exact Chinese title of the document?
  • Does it concern current employment, work experience or the end of employment?
  • Who issued it?
  • Does the employer still exist under the same legal name?
  • Is the available document an original, copy or electronic record?
  • Which dates and position does it show?
  • Is the labour contract available?
  • Are work-permit or cancellation records available?
  • Are tax, social-insurance or payroll records available?
  • What country and receiving organization will use it?
  • What wording and supporting evidence does the recipient require?
  • What language and translation form are required?
  • What is the submission deadline?

Common Mistakes

  • Treating every employment-related document as the same certificate.
  • Translating 离职证明 automatically as proof of voluntary resignation.
  • Assuming company letterhead, seal or HR signature creates Apostille eligibility.
  • Assuming every employment certificate must follow one notarial route.
  • Treating a labour contract as proof that all contracted work occurred.
  • Treating a work permit as proof of duties or performance.
  • Assuming tax or social-insurance records replace an employer letter.
  • Following a universal materials checklist without case review.
  • Assuming remote representation is always accepted.
  • Expecting the Apostille to prove the underlying employment facts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a Chinese employment certificate be Apostilled directly?

Not automatically. A private employer letter may first need a qualifying notarial act or another official certification. Direct eligibility must be confirmed for the actual document.

Does a China employment certificate always need notarization first?

No. A private employment document may require a qualifying notarial act or another competent verification before Apostille, while some eligible documents may follow a direct route. The correct structure depends on the actual document and authority requirements.

Can a Chinese resignation or termination certificate be Apostilled?

Potentially, but its document form and qualifying public or notarial certification must first be identified. The statutory importance of the certificate does not itself establish direct eligibility.

What is the difference between an employment certificate and a resignation or termination certificate?

An employment certificate may confirm current employment or work history. A resignation, separation or termination certificate records the ending of employment. The exact Chinese wording controls.

Can I prepare Chinese employment records after leaving China?

Possibly. A representative may be able to coordinate the process, but authorization, personal-participation and document requirements vary.

What if my former employer refuses to issue a certificate?

Labour-law remedies may exist for a statutory ending certificate. They do not guarantee a bespoke overseas reference, duplicate reissuance or a specific outcome.

Can a labour contract, tax record or work-permit record replace an employment certificate?

Not automatically. These records may support the employment history, but the notarial institution and overseas recipient decide whether they are sufficient.

What if my former company has closed?

Company records, contracts, work permits, tax, social-insurance and payment evidence may assist review. They do not automatically replace the requested employer certificate.

Does the employment document need translation?

It depends on the destination, receiving organization and document structure. The required language and form of translation should be confirmed before preparation; English is not universally required.

Does an Apostille prove that the employment information is true?

No. It authenticates the formal origin of the qualifying public or notarial document, not every underlying employment statement.
