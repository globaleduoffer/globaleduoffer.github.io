China Apostille: Original, Certified Copy or Notarial Copy?

Identify which Chinese document form should enter the Apostille route and what formal origin the Apostille actually authenticates.

By Global Attest Document Services Team · Published: September 1, 2026
Direct Answer: There is no universal rule requiring you to Apostille either the original Chinese document or a copy. The correct form depends on whether the original is itself an accepted public document, whether China-side notarization or another official certification is required, and whether the overseas recipient requests an original, certified copy or foreign-related notarial certificate. An ordinary photocopy is not automatically equivalent to the original. If a copy-conformity certification or notarial certificate receives the Apostille, the Apostille authenticates the formal origin of that certification or notarial act; it does not automatically authenticate the underlying original as though the Apostille were attached directly to it. Recipient acceptance must be confirmed separately.

Sources reviewed: 1 September 2026. China MFA procedures, accepted document forms, notarial requirements and overseas recipient instructions can change. Confirm current requirements before submission.

Original, Certified Copy or Notarial Copy: What Is the Difference?

Before deciding what to submit, identify the document form precisely.

Document formWhat it meansMain caution
Original Chinese public documentAn officially issued original that may qualify as a public documentNot every original is directly eligible
Ordinary photocopyAn applicant-made reproduction without official certificationIt is not automatically a public document
Certified copyA copy certified by an identified authorityAsk who certified it and what was certified
Copy-conformity notarial certificateA notarial instrument confirming that a copy conforms to the originalIt is separate from the underlying source document
Foreign-related notarial certificateA notarial instrument issued for foreign use concerning a document, fact, signature, copy, translation or another matterIt is not simply another name for the original
Scan or PDFA digital reproduction unless officially issued or certified electronicallyIt is not the same as a native electronic public document

“Notarized copy” and “Certified True Copy” can be ambiguous. Safer descriptions include copy-conformity certification and notarial certification that a copy conforms to the original.

Which Document Actually Receives the Apostille?

The central question is:

What public or official certification is the Apostille attached or logically associated with?

The HCCH Apostille Convention applies to public documents. These can include administrative and judicial documents, notarial acts and certain official certifications. Whether a particular instrument is public depends on the law of its place of origin.

ScenarioDocument structureFormal origin authenticated
1Accepted original public document → ApostilleThe issuing official or authority on the original
2Source document → official copy certification → ApostilleThe accepted certification or certified-copy instrument
3Source document → copy-conformity notarial certificate → ApostilleThe notarial act
4Ordinary photocopy without certificationNo independent official origin is established
5Native electronic public document → accepted electronic routeThe qualifying electronic issuing authority

Underlying source document ≠ official copy certification ≠ foreign-related notarial certificate ≠ Apostille

Physical attachment alone does not make every page or enclosure independently authenticated. For more on document structure, see whether copies or attachments need separate Apostilles.

What Does the Apostille Prove?

For a qualifying public document, the Apostille authenticates the authenticity of the signature, the capacity in which the signer acted and the identity of the seal or stamp where applicable.

For a notarial certificate or official copy certification, it authenticates the corresponding formal origin of that notarial or certifying act.

It does not automatically prove every underlying fact, confirm that information remains current, make an ordinary photocopy legally equivalent to the original, authenticate unrelated attachments, verify translation accuracy or guarantee overseas acceptance. The HCCH Apostille Handbook, Second Edition provides detailed guidance on these formal-origin boundaries.

Can an Original Chinese Document Be Apostilled Directly?

Potentially, but not automatically.

An original Chinese public document may qualify where it has public-document status; its issuing authority and formal origin can be authenticated; its signature, capacity, seal or stamp is acceptable; the competent Apostille authority accepts that issued form; and no document-specific prior certification requirement applies.

Current China MFA application guidance contemplates foreign-related notarial certificates and other qualifying certified documents while recognizing exceptions for documents that may receive an Apostille directly. It does not publish a universal rule making every original directly eligible.

If an original is difficult to replace, it can be sensible as a practical matter to ask whether an accepted certified or notarial form is available before committing the original to a particular route. This is planning advice—not a rule that originals should never be Apostilled.

Can a Photocopy Receive a China Apostille?

A plain photocopy is not automatically Apostille-ready merely because the original document could potentially qualify.

An applicant-made photocopy generally has no independent official signature, seal, certification or verifiable public origin. It may need certification by a competent authority or notary, but that does not mean every photocopy can become eligible through notarization.

The document, available evidence, requested certification and notarial-office requirements must all be reviewed.

What Is Copy-Conformity Certification?

China’s PRC Notarization Law permits notarization concerning whether a document’s copy or photocopy conforms to the original.

Original source → notarial certification that the copy conforms to the original → Apostille

In this structure, the Apostille may authenticate the notary’s signature, official capacity and notarial-office seal. It does not independently investigate whether every statement in the source document is true or treat the certified copy as the original itself.

“Certified copy” should therefore not be used without explaining who performed the certification and what proposition was certified.

What Is a Chinese Foreign-Related Notarial Certificate?

A Chinese foreign-related notarial certificate is a separately issued notarial instrument prepared for foreign use. Depending on the notarial matter and supporting evidence, it may concern a document or legally significant fact, copy-original conformity, a signature or seal, translation, or another permissible matter.

China MFA’s foreign-related notarial guidance advises applicants to identify the destination, intended use and any special recipient requirements when requesting the certificate.

Underlying source document ≠ foreign-related notarial certificate ≠ Apostille

Understanding the difference between Apostille and notarization is important: the notarial certificate is the issued instrument, while the Apostille authenticates its formal origin for Convention use.

If you already have an older certificate, the separate issue is whether an older notarial certificate can receive its first Apostille.

Does Apostilling a Certified Copy Authenticate the Original?

Not as though the Apostille were attached directly to the original.

Where a copy certification is the qualifying public document, the Apostille authenticates the formal origin of that certification. It does not independently authenticate the underlying source document, confirm every fact in it or require the overseas recipient to accept the copy.

The exact certification wording matters because it defines what the certifying official or notary has formally confirmed.

Original vs Copy Examples

Personal Documents

Birth, marriage, divorce, death and police records can exist as different original, extract, certified or notarial forms. A medical record, civil-status certificate, court judgment and notarial certificate do not have the same issuing origin. No single original-versus-copy rule can safely be applied to all personal documents.

Academic Documents

Under the current China MFA guidance reviewed for this article, a Graduation Certificate (毕业证书), Degree Certificate (学位证书) and Academic Transcript (成绩单) issued by a Chinese educational institution must first undergo notarization in China before Apostille or consular legalization rather than being submitted directly in their school-issued form.

This academic rule should not be extrapolated to every Chinese document. CHSI/CSSD verification and credential evaluation are separate from Apostille.

Commercial Documents

A Business Licence, registry record, company resolution, Power of Attorney, agreement and Certificate of Origin may have different formal origins. Depending on the document, the route may involve an administrative record, registry certification, notarial act, CCPIT certification or another competent-authority certification. CCPIT is not the universal route for every company document.

Court Documents

A judgment, ruling, mediation statement, court-certified copy, evidence of legal effect and related notarial certificate may represent different issued forms. Although judicial documents fall within the Convention’s public-document framework, that does not establish that every Chinese court original is directly eligible. China-side intake and the recipient’s required judicial-document form must both be checked.

What About Scans, PDFs and Electronic Documents?

Native electronic public document ≠ applicant-created scan of a paper document.

A PDF reproduction does not automatically become Apostille-eligible because the paper original might qualify. An electronically issued document requires a recognized official origin and an accepted electronic authentication route.

China MFA’s current e-Apostille notice began with specified electronic Certificates of Origin issued by CCPIT. That pilot does not establish a universal e-Apostille route for all documents or make applicant-created scans electronic public documents.

Does the Overseas Recipient Accept a Certified or Notarial Copy?

Apostille eligibility ≠ recipient acceptance.

The receiving organization may request an original or recent official extract, an officially certified copy, a copy-conformity notarial certificate, a notarial certificate concerning the underlying fact, a certified translation, electronic verification, direct institutional delivery or a specified number of original sets.

China MFA or an authorized local Foreign Affairs Office decides whether it can issue an Apostille for the submitted qualifying form. It does not determine what every foreign court, university, registry, immigration authority or employer must accept.

China MFA’s destination guidance recommends identifying the destination, purpose and recipient requirements before the document is prepared. If a completed document has already been rejected, review what to check if an Apostilled document is not accepted overseas.

China Apostille Document-Form Review Checklist

  • What is the document’s exact official name?
  • Which authority issued it?
  • Do you hold an original, copy, scan or electronic record?
  • Has it already been notarized or officially certified?
  • What exactly does the existing certification confirm?
  • Where will the document be used?
  • Which organization will receive it?
  • What is the intended filing purpose?
  • Does the recipient specify an original, certified copy or notarial form?
  • What is the filing deadline, if any?

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming every original can proceed directly to Apostille.
  • Assuming every Chinese document must be notarized.
  • Treating a plain photocopy as a certified copy.
  • Saying “certified copy” without identifying the certifier.
  • Assuming Apostille of a copy certification authenticates the original itself.
  • Treating a scan as a native electronic public document.
  • Ignoring the recipient’s document-form instructions.
  • Expecting an Apostille to guarantee acceptance.

China MFA’s refusal guidance also identifies signature, seal, binding and document-format issues that can prevent issuance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I Apostille the original Chinese document or a copy?

It depends on the document’s public status, China-side intake requirements and the overseas recipient’s requested form. Neither the original nor a copy is universally correct.

Can an original Chinese document be Apostilled directly?

Potentially, but direct eligibility is not automatic. The issuing authority, formal signature or seal and any document-specific certification rule must be checked.

Can an ordinary photocopy of a Chinese document be Apostilled?

A plain applicant-made photocopy is not automatically a public document. An accepted official or notarial certification may be required.

What is copy-conformity certification for a China Apostille?

It is an official or notarial certification that a copy or photocopy conforms to the original. If Apostilled, the formal origin of that certification is authenticated.

Is a certified copy the same as a notarized copy in China?

Not necessarily. “Certified copy” does not identify the certifier, while “notarized copy” is also ambiguous. The specific authority, notarial matter and certification wording should be identified.

What is a Chinese foreign-related notarial certificate?

It is a separately issued notarial instrument for foreign use concerning a document, fact, copy, signature, translation or another permissible matter.

Does an Apostille on a certified copy authenticate the original document?

It authenticates the formal origin of the qualifying copy certification. It does not authenticate the source as though the Apostille were attached directly to the original.

Can a scanned PDF receive a China Apostille?

Not automatically. A scan is different from a native or officially certified electronic public document and must have a recognized formal origin and accepted route.

Will an overseas authority accept a certified or notarial copy instead of the original?

Possibly, but acceptance depends on the receiving authority’s rules. An Apostille does not require the recipient to accept a copy.
