Overview

Do You Need a China PCC for Canada?

A China police certificate may form part of your Canadian application when your residence history and the relevant checklist call for it. Confirm the requested period, issue date, format and deadline before starting.

For Express Entry, distinguish the requirements for a country where you currently live from one where you previously lived. Other programs may use different instructions.

Document Preparation

Required Documents: Start With Your China Records

This is a preparation guide, not a nationwide mandatory checklist. Requirements depend on nationality, city, residence period and the local authority’s review.

Identity & Travel History

  • Current passport bio page.
  • Former passport used during your stay in China, if available.
  • Chinese visas, entry/exit stamps and residence permits, as applicable.
  • Evidence explaining a name change or passport-number mismatch.

Residence & Authorization

  • Temporary Residence Registration Form, where applicable.
  • Dates and addresses in each Chinese city.
  • Work or school records that may help locate archives.
  • Power of attorney or other authorization, where permitted.
  • Photos or fingerprints only if the local procedure requests them.

Chinese citizens and former foreign residents follow different routes. Chinese citizens may need identity, hukou and local residence records. Check the relevant part of the IRCC China instructions and the issuing office’s current requirements.

Applying From Overseas

Tell us your former cities, residence dates, current country and available documents. We review the local route before preparing authorization or coordinating a representative. Remote processing is not available through the same procedure everywhere.

Allow for identity checks, archive searches, local attendance requirements and document delivery. See applying after leaving China.

Translation, Notarization & Authentication

Translation communicates the document in another language. Notarization records the specific notarial act. An apostille authenticates origin-related matters; it does not verify the underlying facts or assure acceptance.

Confirm what the actual Canadian recipient needs. Do not assume that translation, notarization, apostille and consular legalization are interchangeable or all mandatory. View PCC format samples and document legalization services.

Your Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a China PCC for Canadian permanent residence?

It depends on your application and residence history. For Express Entry, IRCC generally requests certificates for applicants and family members aged 18 or older for countries where they spent six consecutive months or more within the last ten years, excluding Canada and time before age 18. An officer may request additional certificates. Follow your own checklist.

Does IRCC require a police certificate from China for every application?

No single checklist covers all Canadian applications. Permanent residence, sponsorship, citizenship and some temporary residence applications can involve police certificate requests. A work or study permit does not automatically mean that everyone must obtain a China PCC. Read the instructions and any request letter for your particular file.

Can I apply after leaving China?

An overseas application may be possible, subject to the former Chinese city’s procedure, records and authorization requirements. Some cities support online or representative applications. We assess the available route and assist with preparation and coordination; we cannot promise remote processing in every city.

What documents are commonly required?

A route review may involve your current and former passports, Chinese visas, residence permits, temporary residence registration, address history and work or study records. An authorization letter, photos or fingerprints may be requested locally. Your IRCC checklist helps clarify the purpose and deadline. The final list is city-specific.

Does IRCC require translation or notarization?

IRCC’s China instructions call for a notarized or certified translation. Check the translation format and source-document submission rules for your application. Translation, notarization and authentication are different steps; arranging one does not automatically satisfy every requirement.

Do I need an apostille or embassy legalization?

An apostille is not an automatic requirement for every Canadian immigration file. Confirm whether the actual recipient requests authentication. An apostille concerns the origin of a public document, such as its signature, seal and signing capacity; it does not certify the truth of its contents or guarantee acceptance. If required, the applicable route depends on the document and Convention relationship.

How long does the application take?

There is no reliable nationwide completion time. The estimate depends on the city, residence period, available records, identity checks and authority workload. Authorization, archive searches, translation, notarization and delivery can add time. We provide a case-specific estimate after review, without guaranteeing a date or outcome.

What if I no longer have my old passport, visa or residence records?

Send details of what remains available before ordering replacement documents. Copies, previous passport numbers, registration records, employment or school records and address history may help a local review. These are not automatically accepted substitutes, and some cases cannot proceed without the required evidence.

What if I never held a Chinese residence permit?

IRCC’s China page states that the certificate for foreign nationals is available to current or former Foreigner Residence Permit holders. A visitor visa is not the same document. Ask the relevant local authority to assess your records rather than assuming a certificate can be issued.

What if the Chinese authorities cannot issue my PCC?

Do not replace it with an unsupported guarantee or fabricated document. Follow IRCC’s China instructions for explaining a missing certificate or period. Where an Express Entry deadline cannot be met, provide an explanation and evidence of efforts as directed. IRCC decides whether the evidence is sufficient; an explanation is not an automatic exemption.
