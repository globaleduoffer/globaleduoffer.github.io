Can an Old Chinese Notarial Certificate Be Apostilled?

Review whether an older or pre-2023 Chinese foreign-related notarial certificate may still enter the Apostille route before paying for a new notarization.

By Global Attest Document Services Team · Published: August 30, 2026
Direct answer: Possibly. An older Chinese notarial certificate is not automatically disqualified under the HCCH framework merely because of its age, and reviewed national China MFA guidance does not publish a blanket rule prohibiting certificates issued before 7 November 2023. However, the certificate must still satisfy the competent Chinese authority’s requirements, including its original form, signature, seal, condition and applicable timing rules. The underlying document or certified fact and the overseas recipient’s freshness requirements must also be checked. An Apostille authenticates formal origin; it does not update outdated information or guarantee overseas acceptance.

Can an Old Chinese Notarial Certificate Still Receive an Apostille?

An older certificate may still be suitable for review, but there is no safe rule that every old certificate qualifies—or that every old certificate must be replaced.

The competent Chinese authority must be able to accept and authenticate the certificate’s formal origin. The destination and overseas receiving organization must also accept the document type, form and age.

If you need the broader route first, review the China Apostille requirements and process. This article focuses only on whether an existing notarial certificate may be reused instead of newly issued.

Does a Chinese Notarial Certificate Expire?

No universal maximum age for every mainland Chinese foreign-related notarial certificate was found in the reviewed national MFA and notarization sources. This does not mean that every certificate remains suitable indefinitely: document-specific validity, certified facts, authority intake rules and recipient freshness requirements remain separate questions.

The PRC Notarization Law states that a notarial certificate takes effect when issued and provides procedures for correcting or revoking certificates with specified problems. The MOJ Notarization Procedure Rules also address certificate issuance, correction and revocation.

These provisions should not be simplified into “notarization never expires.” The certificate’s legal status, the continuing relevance of what it certifies and the recipient’s acceptance rules may produce different answers.

It is also important to understand the difference between Apostille and notarization. A notarial certificate does not become Apostilled simply because it remains an issued certificate.

Five Separate Questions to Check

Certificate status

First determine whether the certificate remains complete and has not been corrected, replaced or revoked. Its issue date is relevant, but age alone does not answer this question.

Underlying document or certified fact

Identify what the notarial certificate actually certifies. It might certify a historical document, a copy, a signature, a translation or a fact that could have changed since issuance.

A certificate can remain physically intact while the information needed by the overseas recipient is no longer sufficiently current.

Original and document condition

Current China MFA Apostille application instructions refer to submitting the original of a valid document. Review whether the existing certificate is an original, whether all pages remain intact, and whether its signatures, seals and binding are complete.

The China Apostille and notarial certificate samples can help illustrate how a notarial package and later Apostille may appear, but a sample cannot confirm eligibility for a particular old certificate.

Chinese authority intake

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs or an authorized local Foreign Affairs Office must accept the document for the relevant route. The authority may need to verify its signature, seal, issuing origin and documentary form.

China MFA’s refusal guidance identifies circumstances in which problems with signatures, seals, binding or applicable timing requirements may prevent Apostille issuance. It does not establish that age alone resolves every case.

Overseas recipient freshness

The overseas organization may require a recently issued certificate, a current extract, a particular translation or a different notarial form. Its rules are separate from the Chinese authority’s Apostille intake decision.

What If the Certificate Was Issued Before 7 November 2023?

The Apostille Convention took effect for China on 7 November 2023, as confirmed by the China MFA implementation notice and the HCCH status table.

That date marks the beginning of China’s Convention-based Apostille route. It does not, by itself, establish that earlier Chinese notarial certificates are unusable.

A notarial certificate’s pre-7 November 2023 issue date is not, by itself, a verified automatic bar under the HCCH framework. China-specific acceptance of the particular certificate still needs confirmation because national MFA guidance does not publish a blanket rule for pre-2023 certificates.

Applicants should therefore avoid both extremes: assuming every pre-2023 certificate qualifies and replacing every such certificate without first reviewing it.

Old Notarial Certificate vs Old Underlying Document

An old notarial certificate is not the same issue as an old underlying document or an old certified fact.

Three dates may matter:

  1. The notarial certificate’s issue date.
  2. The date or period covered by the underlying record.
  3. The overseas recipient’s permitted freshness window.

For example, a certificate issued years ago may concern an even older academic award. Alternatively, it may concern marital or company status as it existed only on a particular date.

The notarial certificate’s issue date, the date or period covered by the underlying record, and the recipient’s freshness window are three different dates.

Historical Facts vs Information That Can Change

Some records concern events that normally remain historical. A degree records an academic award, while a birth record concerns a past event. Their age does not automatically make the recorded event disappear, although the recipient may still require a specified format or recent extract.

Other documents concern information that can change:

  • A police certificate normally covers a stated date or period.
  • Marital status can change.
  • Employment status can change.
  • Company registration, representation or operating status can change.

These documents do not share one universal freshness period. The recipient’s current instructions and the exact fact certified must be reviewed.

When Might a New Notarization Be Necessary?

A new notarization may need to be considered where:

  • the certificate’s formal origin cannot be verified;
  • the signature or seal cannot satisfy authority verification;
  • the existing format does not match the current route or recipient request;
  • the certificate is damaged, incomplete or detached;
  • the recipient requires a recently issued document;
  • the underlying fact must be updated;
  • a different original, certified-copy or notarial form is required;
  • required formal elements are missing;
  • the translation or language arrangement no longer matches the request; or
  • the existing certificate certifies the wrong fact, copy or signature for the current use.

These are possible review outcomes, not automatic re-notarization rules.

If several existing certificates are involved, separately check whether separate notarial certificates need separate Apostilles.

Can the Signature, Seal and Issuing Origin Still Be Verified?

The HCCH Apostille Handbook explains that age alone does not remove a document’s public nature unless the law or policy of the State of origin provides otherwise. It also recognizes that verifying the origin of an old document can become practically difficult.

If the competent authority cannot verify the signature, seal or issuing origin of an older certificate, another officially certified or newly notarized form may need to be considered.

This does not mean that retirement of a notary, an office-name change or absence from an online system automatically invalidates the certificate. Those facts require case-specific verification.

Does an Apostille Renew or Extend Document Validity?

No.

Under the HCCH Apostille Convention, an Apostille authenticates formal-origin elements associated with the qualifying public document. It does not extend the validity period of the underlying document or make outdated information current.

An old police-related fact does not become current merely because the certificate receives an Apostille. The same applies to marital, employment or company status that may have changed.

This article concerns an existing notarial certificate that has not yet received its first Apostille. An Apostille already issued is a different question. HCCH guidance does not impose an age-based expiration on a validly issued Apostille merely because time passes, but the underlying document may still face recipient freshness rules.

Existing-Certificate Review Checklist

Before obtaining a replacement certificate, collect:

  • the complete existing notarial certificate;
  • clear copies of every page;
  • document issue date and issuing location;
  • the underlying document or record;
  • destination country;
  • intended use;
  • overseas recipient’s current instructions;
  • required language or translation form;
  • required number of originals; and
  • the application deadline.

If the certificate has already received an Apostille but is being rejected, follow the separate guide on what to check if an Apostilled document is not accepted overseas.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming every old certificate is invalid.
  • Assuming every old certificate remains usable.
  • Confusing certificate age with underlying-document freshness.
  • Applying a universal six-month rule.
  • Assuming every pre-2023 certificate must be replaced.
  • Paying for new notarization before reviewing the existing certificate.
  • Assuming an Apostille renews outdated information.
  • Ignoring recipient freshness requirements.
  • Submitting a damaged or incomplete certificate.
  • Treating Apostille eligibility as guaranteed overseas acceptance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an old Chinese notarial certificate be Apostilled?

Possibly. Its age alone is not an automatic HCCH-level bar, but its original form, condition, formal origin, applicable Chinese intake requirements and overseas recipient rules must be checked.

Do Chinese notarial certificates expire?

There is no verified universal maximum age that applies to every mainland Chinese foreign-related notarial certificate. That does not mean every certificate can be used indefinitely: the underlying fact, document-specific requirements, Chinese authority intake and overseas recipient freshness rules still matter.

Is there a six-month limit for every Chinese notarial certificate?

No universal six-month rule was found. Some recipients or document types may impose their own periods.

Can a certificate issued before 7 November 2023 receive an Apostille?

It may be eligible for review. Its earlier issue date is not automatically disqualifying under the HCCH framework, but China-specific authority acceptance must be confirmed.

Must every pre-2023 certificate be re-notarized?

No blanket national rule requiring this was found. Re-notarization may be needed because of form, verification, condition, underlying information or recipient requirements.

Does the age of the underlying document matter?

It can. Historical records and documents concerning current or changeable facts may be treated differently.

Can an old police or marital-status notarial certificate still be used?

Possibly, but these records may face recipient-specific freshness requirements because the relevant period or status matters.

What if the old notarial certificate’s signature or seal cannot be verified?

Another officially certified or newly notarized form may need to be considered, subject to the competent authority’s instructions.

Does an Apostille extend the validity of the underlying document?

No. It authenticates formal origin and does not renew the underlying document or update its information.

Does Apostille eligibility guarantee overseas acceptance?

No. The receiving organization decides whether the document’s type, age, content, translation and form satisfy its requirements.
