What to Do If a China Police Certificate Cannot Be Issued

A China police certificate may be difficult to obtain when historical residence records, old passport details or local city records cannot be matched. The practical first step is to identify the missing period and understand why the usual route is not working.

By Global Attest Document Services Team · Published: August 11, 2026
Important: A document that explains an unavailable police certificate is not automatically a substitute for the certificate. The destination authority or receiving organization decides what it will accept.

Some former residents of China discover a problem only after an immigration, visa, employment or licensing authority asks for a police certificate covering a specific period. The applicant may be unable to locate the records needed by the issuing city, or the local route may not produce a certificate for the full period requested.

“Cannot be issued” does not always describe the same situation. It may mean that one residence period cannot be verified, that an old passport cannot be matched to current identity details, or that the applicant has not yet approached the correct city or authority. Before preparing an explanation, first determine why the China Police Certificate route appears unavailable. For broader terminology and document types, see the China No Criminal Record Certificate guide.

Why a China Police Certificate May Be Unavailable

China PCC practice can vary by city, residence period and the records available to the local authority. The following are possible causes, not nationwide rules:

  • Historical residence records are incomplete or cannot be matched to the requested period.
  • A temporary residence registration record cannot be located.
  • The applicant lived in China many years ago and only limited records remain available.
  • Old passport details differ from the current passport.
  • Name spelling, spacing or order differs across passport, visa and local records.
  • The residence history covers more than one Chinese city.
  • Local issuing practice or the responsible office has changed.
  • Supporting immigration, work, study or accommodation records are incomplete.

One missing document does not lead to an identical outcome in every city. The useful question is not only whether a PCC can be obtained, but which period, identity record or city cannot currently be verified.

First Identify Which Residence Period Is Missing

Build a residence timeline before contacting an issuing office or preparing a letter for the receiving authority. Separate each city and each passport period. This often reveals whether the problem affects the full China residence history or only one part of it.

Check Your Old Passport and China Residence Records

Historical materials may help establish or reconstruct the residence history. Depending on the case, useful records may include:

  • Old passports and copies of their information pages.
  • China visa pages and entry stamps.
  • Residence permits and work permits.
  • University enrollment, graduation or student-status records.
  • Temporary residence registration forms or accommodation records.
  • Employment contracts, tax records or employer letters.
  • Entry-exit records or a reliable timeline prepared from other evidence.

These materials are not automatically mandatory in every city, and possessing them does not guarantee that a police certificate will be issued. Their value is that they may help identify the correct period, city, passport and local record.

What If Your Temporary Residence Registration Is Missing?

A Temporary Residence Registration Form records an address used during a stay in China. In some cases it may help connect the applicant to a city and residence period. However, cities and historical periods do not all apply the same record requirements.

If the original form is unavailable, first check whether copies were retained by a hotel, landlord, employer, university or former immigration file. Other evidence may also help explain the address history, but the issuing route still depends on local practice. You can see examples of China Temporary Residence Registration Forms to understand common document layouts without assuming that one sample is mandatory nationwide.

What If You Used an Old Passport?

Many former residents lived in China under a passport that has since expired or been replaced. The current passport number may therefore differ from the number shown on a historical visa, residence permit, accommodation registration, employment record or university record.

This difference does not automatically mean that a PCC cannot be obtained. The practical issue is identity continuity: whether the available documents can connect the current applicant to the older passport and the relevant China residence period. The guide to passport and residence record mismatches explains how to organize these differences. Name changes, transliteration differences and missing middle names should be identified early rather than left unexplained.

If the old passport itself is unavailable, gather any copies, visa records or institutional records that still show the former passport details. The city or receiving authority determines whether the available evidence is sufficient for its purpose.

What If You Lived in More Than One Chinese City?

Do not assume that a certificate connected to one city automatically covers all residence elsewhere in China. Historical records may be held or assessed locally, and different cities may require separate review. A multi-city residence timeline can help separate the records and missing periods for each location.

List each city, the exact or approximate dates, the passport used and the records available for that location. Then compare the requested coverage period with the city history. The China Police Certificate requirements by city provide city-specific starting points, but current local practice should still be confirmed for the individual case.

If the Police Certificate Still Cannot Be Obtained

If reasonable enquiries have been made and the requested PCC still cannot be obtained, avoid presenting an unverified document as a replacement. A more careful next step is to document the situation and ask the receiving authority what its current procedure allows.

  1. Record the enquiries and attempts made.
  2. Keep relevant correspondence and supporting records.
  3. Identify the specific city, period or record problem.
  4. Check the receiving authority’s current police-certificate instructions.
  5. Ask whether an explanation or other evidence may be considered where permitted.

A Letter of Explanation may help describe the circumstances, but it is not guaranteed to replace a police certificate. Some authorities may request further evidence, give different instructions or decide that the available explanation is insufficient.

What Evidence May Help Explain the Situation?

The following records may help explain why a certificate is unavailable or show that reasonable efforts were made:

  • Copies of current and old passports.
  • China visas and residence permits.
  • Temporary residence records, if available.
  • Employer, tax, work-permit or university records.
  • Correspondence showing attempts to obtain the PCC.
  • Any written response received from the relevant authority.
  • A clear city-by-city residence timeline.

Describe each record accurately. Do not label a private letter or general correspondence as an “alternative police certificate” unless it is actually an official document with that status.

Does the Destination Country Matter?

Yes. Canada, the United States, Australia, New Zealand and other authorities may apply different residence thresholds, document formats and procedures when a police certificate cannot be obtained. Requirements can also vary by visa category, immigration program, employer or professional regulator.

Do not rely on another applicant’s outcome as a universal rule. Review the relevant authority’s current instructions and use the China Police Certificate requirements by destination country to locate the appropriate country guidance without duplicating every national rule here.

When to Ask for Case-Specific Review

A case-specific review may be useful when the applicant lived in China many years ago, used several passports, lived in multiple cities, cannot locate accommodation registration, has a name mismatch, faces an approaching immigration deadline or is unsure whether notarization or authentication is required.

Prepare the residence timeline first, then send only the basic case details through the Global Attest contact page. A review can help identify the likely next enquiry or missing record, but it cannot guarantee PCC issuance or acceptance by the destination authority.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a China police certificate always be obtained?

No. Availability may depend on the city, residence period, local records, passport history and current issuing practice. A case should be reviewed before any conclusion is reached.

What if I no longer have my old passport?

Other records such as visa copies, residence permits, employment or university records may help reconstruct the residence history, but whether they are sufficient depends on the city and the case.

What if my temporary residence registration is missing?

A missing registration form does not produce the same result in every case. The city, residence period and other available records should be checked before deciding whether a PCC route remains possible.

Do I need police certificates from every city where I lived in China?

Requirements vary. Different city records may need separate assessment, and a certificate from one city should not automatically be assumed to cover residence in another city.

What should I do if the receiving authority asks for a China PCC that I cannot obtain?

Keep evidence of the attempts made, identify why the certificate appears unavailable and check the receiving authority’s current instructions. An explanation or other evidence may be considered only where that authority permits it.
