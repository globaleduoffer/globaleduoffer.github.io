WES Rejected Your Chinese Academic Documents or Asked for More? What to Check

Identify whether a WES document problem concerns the document type, submission route, CSSD/CHSI verification, university records, identity information or translation.

By Global Attest Document Services Team · Published: September 5, 2026
Direct answer: A WES document problem does not automatically mean that your Chinese degree or other credential has received a final negative evaluation.

WES reviews and, where necessary, verifies submitted documents before completing credential analysis and producing its final report. If something is missing, incomplete, incorrectly submitted or awaiting verification, the file may stop at the document-review stage.

“WES rejected my documents” is common applicant language, but WES may use more specific wording, including waiting for documents, reviewing documents, waiting for institution verification, additional documents required, documents incomplete, documents not accepted in their present form or file on hold.

Start by reading the exact message in your WES account. The next step depends on whether the issue concerns the document type, submission route, CSSD/CHSI verification, university records, identity information, translation or document quality.

If you need assistance diagnosing the notice, Global Attest provides China academic document support for WES. The final decision about document acceptance and credential evaluation remains with WES.

What Did WES Actually Say?

Do not choose a remedy based only on the word “rejected.” Save a screenshot or copy the complete WES message. Check:

  • which credential the message identifies;
  • which document is missing or unacceptable;
  • whether WES is waiting for you or an institution;
  • whether the file is awaiting verification;
  • whether WES gives a new submission instruction;
  • whether a deadline or reference number appears; and
  • whether the notice concerns one document or the entire application.

WES’s official explanation of its credential-evaluation process separates document review, missing-document handling, secondary verification, credential analysis and final report production.

For general application steps, use the step-by-step WES guide for Chinese credentials. This article focuses on what to check after a document problem has already appeared.

Does “Documents Not Accepted” Mean the Degree Was Rejected?

Not necessarily.

A document may be unacceptable in its current form because it is incomplete, illegible, sent through the wrong channel or missing required source verification. That is different from a final conclusion about the validity or equivalency of the credential.

A document-status problem means WES cannot yet complete the relevant stage using the information currently accepted in the file. The exact WES message should control the response.

  • waiting for documents does not equal a final negative evaluation;
  • waiting for institution verification does not equal a final negative evaluation;
  • an additional-document request does not mean the application has permanently failed; and
  • a document that cannot be accepted in its submitted form does not automatically prove the degree is invalid.

Identify Which Chinese Academic Document WES Is Asking About

First determine the exact document or record involved. Possible items include:

  • degree certificate;
  • graduation certificate or qualification credential;
  • academic transcript;
  • qualification verification report;
  • degree verification report;
  • transcript verification report;
  • translation; or
  • identity or name-change evidence.

Compare the WES message with the document names shown in your account. Do not assume that one academic document can substitute for another.

Degree Certificate vs Graduation or Qualification Certificate vs Transcript

A Chinese degree certificate and graduation certificate are not interchangeable. A graduation certificate generally concerns completion of the academic program and the resulting higher-education qualification. A degree certificate concerns the academic degree awarded. A transcript records courses, grades or other academic results.

CSSD likewise distinguishes qualification, degree and transcript verification. Applicants can review the available CHSI degree, qualification and transcript verification reports before deciding which record a WES notice concerns.

Submitting a degree certificate when WES requested a graduation or qualification record may leave the requirement unsatisfied. The same applies if an ordinary transcript is confused with a transcript-verification report.

Check Your WES Required Documents List

The controlling source is your current WES Required Documents instructions and the notices in your account.

Requirements can vary by credential, institution, country of education, evaluation type, application purpose, document type and verification route.

Do not rely only on an old checklist, another applicant’s case or general advice found online. Confirm every requested item and note who must send it.

Do not send extra documents merely because they seem potentially useful. Unrequested material may not address the specific deficiency WES identified and may not be reviewed in the way you expect.

Check Who Must Send or Deliver the Document

A WES problem may concern how the document arrived rather than the credential itself. For example:

  • a transcript may have been uploaded through a function intended for degree certificates or translations;
  • WES may require a document from an institution or approved verification source;
  • a paper envelope may not meet the applicable sealing requirements;
  • an electronic message may come from a source WES cannot verify; or
  • the WES reference number may be missing or incorrect.

WES’s current upload guidance distinguishes degree-certificate or translation uploads from academic records that must follow another official route.

Do not assume that every transcript must be sent directly by the university. For some Chinese credentials, an approved CSSD verification and delivery route may apply. Follow the instructions generated for your case.

Check CSSD or CHSI Verification and Report Delivery

WES recognizes CSSD routes for certain credentials from Chinese higher-education institutions. Depending on the applicable requirement, CSSD may provide qualification verification, degree verification, transcript verification or electronic report delivery to WES.

The official CSSD instructions for WES describe separate report categories. They do not establish that every applicant needs all three reports or that every Chinese credential follows the same route.

Check whether you obtained the correct report type, whether it relates to the credential entered in WES, whether the identity information is consistent, whether the correct WES reference information was used and whether the report was delivered through the required channel.

A CSSD report can be part of the required evidence, but it does not guarantee WES acceptance or a particular equivalency result.

What If the Required CSSD or CHSI Report Is Unavailable?

First determine why the report is unavailable. The underlying academic record may be missing, the wrong report type may have been selected, historical records may require another verification route, or an account or identity issue may be preventing access.

If the underlying degree or qualification record cannot be found, use the dedicated guide for troubleshooting when a China degree record is not found on CHSI.

Do not conclude that WES evaluation is automatically impossible. The consequence depends on what WES requires and whether CSSD, the university or WES identifies another official route for that case.

Check University Source Records and Verification

A university-related problem may involve:

  • an incomplete transcript;
  • missing pages, courses or grades;
  • incorrect academic information;
  • a discrepancy between the university archive and submitted document;
  • WES waiting for the institution to verify authenticity; or
  • an institution requiring applicant authorization before responding.

WES’s delay guidance confirms that incomplete academic records, illegible documents and institution verification can prevent an evaluation from proceeding.

This section is diagnostic. If the university will not issue, send or verify a required record, the operational solution depends on that institution’s procedures and the exact route required by WES. For focused guidance, see what to check when a Chinese university will not send or verify a transcript for WES.

Check Passport, Name and Identity Differences

If WES cannot match a document or CSSD report to your application, compare the identity entered in the WES application, current passport, passport used while studying, name and date of birth in university records, identity shown on the CSSD report and lawful name-change evidence where relevant.

A difference does not automatically mean the university record is wrong. Historical passport or name information may have been correct when the academic record was created.

For the complete identity-continuity analysis, use the CHSI old-passport and name-mismatch troubleshooting guide.

Check Translation, Legibility and Completeness

Follow the translation instructions in your WES account. Where a translation is required, it should be complete, readable and accurately reflect the original document. It should not omit pages, seals, annotations, course information or other relevant content.

A translation does not replace the required original-language academic document. Applicant-prepared translations should not be treated as automatically acceptable. Current WES translation requirements refer to appropriate institutional or professional translation routes.

Also check that every page is included, names and dates are readable, the scan is not cropped, grades and course titles can be read, the translation corresponds to the correct original and no necessary identifying information is missing.

Not every Chinese document universally requires a certified translation. The applicable WES instructions control.

What Does Secondary Verification Mean?

WES may contact the issuing institution or relevant authority when additional verification is required.

According to the official WES verification guidance, verification may occur when documents differ from those normally received from the institution, when information does not correspond to the application, or when source confirmation is required.

Secondary verification should not automatically be described as an allegation of fraud. It means WES needs confirmation before proceeding.

Monitor your WES account and email. The institution may require applicant authorization, additional information or its own administrative steps before responding. Follow the instructions from WES and the institution rather than trying to bypass the verification process.

When Should You Contact WES?

Contact WES when the notice does not identify the missing document clearly, your Required Documents list conflicts with the new message, WES appears not to have matched a delivered CSSD report, you need confirmation of the accepted submission route, your institution says it already answered a verification request, or the status does not reflect documented delivery.

Ask a focused question and include the WES reference number through the secure channel. Do not send sensitive documents publicly.

When Should You Contact the University?

The university is more likely to be relevant when a new or complete transcript is needed, courses or grades are missing, the source academic information appears incorrect, WES has sent a verification request, archive confirmation is required, or the institution must authorize or send a record through the specified route.

University procedures vary. Applicant participation, identity verification, written authorization, fees or original documents may be required. Assistance with coordination does not guarantee that the university will cooperate.

When Should You Troubleshoot CHSI or CSSD First?

Start with CHSI or CSSD when the WES requirement depends on a report that cannot be found or generated, uses the wrong academic-record category, cannot be delivered, contains an identity inconsistency or has not been associated with the WES case.

Start with the university instead if the underlying source academic data appear missing or objectively incorrect.

WES vs Notarization and Apostille

WES evaluation, CSSD/CHSI verification, notarization and Apostille are separate processes.

CSSD/CHSI verifies specified academic information. WES evaluates the credential. Notarization prepares or certifies a document under the applicable notarial procedure. Apostille authenticates the formal origin of a qualifying public or notarial document for Convention use.

Notarization or Apostille should not be presented as a cure for a WES document deficiency unless WES expressly requests that format. An Apostille does not create a WES evaluation, supply a missing transcript or correct academic source data.

Documents to Review Before Asking for Help

Gather the materials relevant to the notice, if available:

  • screenshot or complete text of the WES message;
  • current WES Required Documents list;
  • WES reference number;
  • degree certificate;
  • graduation certificate;
  • transcript;
  • CSSD/CHSI reports;
  • report-delivery evidence;
  • current and historical identity information;
  • translations;
  • university correspondence; and
  • submission or courier evidence.

Not every item is required in every case. Begin with the WES notice and the document it identifies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why did WES ask me for additional Chinese academic documents?

WES may need a missing record, another document type, better-quality material, source verification, a translation or clarification about the credential. Read the exact message and compare it with the current Required Documents list before submitting anything else.

Does “documents not accepted” mean my Chinese degree was rejected?

Not necessarily. The submitted document, format or delivery route may be unacceptable without WES having reached a final conclusion about the credential itself.

Does WES require CHSI or CSSD verification for every Chinese degree?

No universal rule supports that statement. WES recognizes CSSD routes for certain Chinese credentials, but the applicable requirement depends on the credential and current WES instructions.

What if WES says my Chinese university transcript is missing?

Check whether WES requires an ordinary institutional transcript, CSSD transcript verification or another specified route. Then confirm whether the document was sent, delivered and matched to the correct reference number.

Can I upload or send my Chinese university transcript to WES myself?

Only if the applicable WES instructions permit that method. Do not upload a transcript through a function intended for degree certificates or translations. Some records must come through an institution or approved verification source.

What if my university will not send or verify documents for WES?

Confirm first whether university action is actually required. If it is, ask what authorization, fee, archive request or delivery method the institution requires. If university action is genuinely required and the institution cannot use the expected route, ask WES what alternative official route, if any, is acceptable before sending documents another way.

What if the required CHSI or CSSD report is unavailable?

Identify whether the problem concerns a missing academic record, report type, historical verification route, identity mismatch or account function. Ask WES whether another official route is acceptable rather than assuming the evaluation cannot proceed.

Can a passport or name mismatch affect how WES matches my documents?

Yes, it may complicate matching or verification. Compare the WES application, current and historical passports, university data and CSSD report before deciding whether correction or continuity evidence is needed.

Does WES require English translations of Chinese academic documents?

Translation requirements depend on the WES program and account instructions. When required, the translation should be complete and accurate and normally accompanies rather than replaces the original-language document.

Can notarization or Apostille fix a WES document problem?

Not automatically. Notarization and Apostille serve different purposes from WES evaluation and CSSD verification. Use them only where WES or another receiving organization separately requires that route.
